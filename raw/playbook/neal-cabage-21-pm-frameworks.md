# 21 Product Management Frameworks: framework extract

- Source: Neal Cabage (personal site)
- Date: not shown
- Pasted into the project by the user on 2026-11-20
- This file keeps only short paraphrases of what the article says about each framework. The article's own text is not copied here.

- **Double Diamond**: Developed by the British Design Council; iterative, using divergent and convergent thinking.
- **Minimum viable product (MVP)**: Credited to Eric Ries; a bare-bones product to test assumptions and gather feedback; "also called Lean Software Development".
- **Customer journey map**: A visual of all steps a customer takes with the product, "sometimes called the 5 Es"; helps PMs understand behavior and set strategy.
- **RICE scoring**: Developed at Intercom; ranks ideas by a single combined score.
- **Weighted scoring**: Called weighted impact scoring: pick criteria, give each a percentage weight, score and rank.
- **Kano model**: Two axes: how well a need is met and resulting satisfaction; prioritize delight relative to cost.
- **DACI and RACI**: Driver, approver, contributor, informed; similar to RACI but suited to PM because it stresses ownership rather than delivery.
- **Business Model Canvas**: A high-level view of how the product team creates value; often used with the Value Proposition Canvas.
- **Ansoff matrix**: Also called the product-market matrix; Igor Ansoff, 1957; four growth strategies.
- **GIST planning**: Goals, ideas, steps and tasks; by Itamar Gilad; draws on Lean Startup, growth marketing and behavioral economics.
- **North Star framework**: Introduced by Amplitude; quotes Sean Ellis that the metric best captures the core value delivered; complemented by key inputs.
- **Spotify model (squads, tribes, chapters, guilds)**: Autonomous, cross-functional squads choose what to work on toward an outcome, within the overall strategy.
- **jobs-to-be-done**: Focuses on scenarios rather than personas; championed by Clayton Christensen.
- **opportunity-solution-tree**: Developed by Teresa Torres; a visual of how you'll reach a desired outcome, to simplify discovery and prioritize opportunities.
- **working-backwards-pr-faq**: Starts by imagining the product is built; the press release describes the problem and the value above alternatives.
- **circles-method**: A design framework by Lewis Lin for answering design questions.
- **design-sprint**: Created by Jake Knapp at Google Ventures; a five-day process inspired by design thinking that reduces launch risk.
- **hook-model**: From Nir Eyal's 2014 book Hooked; four stages: trigger, action, reward, investment.
- **technology-adoption-lifecycle**: Everett Rogers's innovation adoption curve: innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards; approach each group differently.
- **value-proposition-canvas**: Typically used together with the Business Model Canvas.
- **3 Pillars of Product and Product Team Competencies**: Listed as a framework.
- **5 Es**: One source calls journey maps "sometimes called the 5 Es"; another describes a marketing campaign method: engage, explore, express, exchange, extend.
