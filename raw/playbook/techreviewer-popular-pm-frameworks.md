# The Most Popular Product Management Frameworks: framework extract

- Source: Techreviewer blog
- Date: not shown
- Pasted into the project by the user on 2026-11-20
- This file keeps only short paraphrases of what the article says about each framework. The article's own text is not copied here.

- **Minimum viable product (MVP)**: Build the smallest version that achieves your goals; you can always add features later.
- **Customer journey map**: Journey mapping is listed as a principle of Growth-Driven Design.
- **Eisenhower matrix**: Important and not urgent is "tasks you want to do, but can wait".
- **Business Model Canvas**: Says the canvas has nine building blocks, then lists five.
- **HEART framework**: "Ask yourself four questions", then lists five; defines engagement as how easily users can access the product and retention as user numbers compared with other products.
- **Agile and Scrum**: Suggests Scrum or Kanban might be better options than Agile for organizations that need structure; says Lean Software Development was developed by the Software Engineering Institute in 1997 and formalized in 2003.
- **jobs-to-be-done**: Reframes the problem around what customers need rather than what they want.
- **Waterfall**: Listed as a framework.
- **Storytelling**: Listed as a framework.
- **Sales objectives**: Listed as a framework.
- **RFM (recency, frequency, monetary value)**: Called "the most popular product framework" for prioritizing product development, similar to Kano.
- **REAN**: Described as Reduce, Elevate, Amplify, Nurture, "three essential parts" that are then listed as four.
- **Growth-Driven Design**: Described as "also known as A/B testing or split testing".
- **AIDA(R)**: A marketing funnel: attention, interest, desire, action, retention.
- **5 Es**: One source calls journey maps "sometimes called the 5 Es"; another describes a marketing campaign method: engage, explore, express, exchange, extend.
