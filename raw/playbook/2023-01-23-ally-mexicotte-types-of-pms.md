# Types of Product Managers: Which Specialization Will Ignite Your Career?: extract on PM types

- Source: Ally Mexicotte, Medium
- Date: 2023-01-23
- Pasted into the project by the user on 2026-11-21
- This file keeps only short paraphrases of what the article says about each PM type or role. Salary and compensation content is left out. The article's own text is not copied here.

- **Technical product manager (TPM)**: Sometimes a former engineer, sometimes a PM who loves the technical side; more common for developer-facing products.
- **Growth product manager**: Works on a product that already has product-market fit, owns one growth metric, and needs enough weekly users for statistically sound A/B tests.
- **Platform product manager**: Scales multiple products on a platform, is further from the end user, handles build vs buy, and works at larger companies; titles include trust and security, identity, data and ML PM.
- **Enterprise (B2B) vs consumer (B2C) PM**: Enterprise PMs build for business customers, move slowly because of legal, risk and procurement sign-off, and are unlikely at startups. Consumer PM is the most attractive and most competitive type.
- **Generalist vs specialist PM**: Lumping all PM roles together hides which skills and frameworks work in which context.
- **Product operations**: Supports PMs with structure for interviews, QA, experiments, tools and data; probably only at large companies; she has not met anyone in the role.
- **Product marketing manager (PMM)**: Owns positioning, messaging and go-to-market for existing products and feeds customer feedback back to PMs.
- **Types of product work**: A 0-to-1 PM launches a new product to find fit, with little data and a focus on vision, structure and go-to-market; titles include innovation PM and most startup PM roles.
- **Ecosystem or partner PM**: Listed as a PM type or related role.
- **Other job titles for PMM and consumer PM**: Ally Mexicotte gives the same list (acquisition, activation, retention, pricing PM and so on) for growth, consumer and PMM roles.
- **Product management dates from the 1940s**: Ally Mexicotte.
