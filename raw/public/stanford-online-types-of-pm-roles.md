# Types of Product Management Roles: Finding Your Fit in a Diverse Field: extract on PM types

- Source: Stanford Online, quoting coach Priyanka Upadhyay and author Alex Mitchell
- Date: not shown
- Pasted into the project by the user on 2026-11-21
- This file keeps only short paraphrases of what the article says about each PM type or role. Salary and compensation content is left out. The article's own text is not copied here.

- **Technical product manager (TPM)**: Upadhyay: there is no non-technical PM, but not every PM role needs an engineering degree. Mitchell: a TPM may lack strategic and marketing insight but builds strong relationships with developers.
- **Growth product manager**: Doesn't own a specific product; works on the growth of one or more products alongside the PMs who own them.
- **Platform product manager**: Inbound (internal-facing) PM roles stress stakeholder work, consensus and saying no; internal customers can be harder than external ones because they have more access to the PM.
- **Data product manager**: Mitchell's analytic PM archetype suits former data analysts and scientists who want to drive business change.
- **AI product manager**: A highly technical subspecialty like AI or ML may need an engineering background.
- **Enterprise (B2B) vs consumer (B2C) PM**: In B2C the user and customer are typically the same; in B2B the paying executive differs from the employee user, so the PM balances more stakeholders. Many employers prefer experience in the same model.
- **Generalist vs specialist PM**: A generalist knows the end-to-end lifecycle and can lead teams in any industry or domain; domain or industry expertise can be a superpower in a tough job market.
- **Product marketing manager (PMM)**: Mitchell's marketing PM archetype suits people with advertising, PR or marketing backgrounds.
- **PM archetypes**: Mitchell's five archetypes: technical PM (former developer), analytic PM (former analyst), marketing PM, get-stuff-done PM (high-growth startups, delivery at all costs) and visionary PM (rare, thinks in years).
- **Domain or industry PM**: Listed as a PM type or related role.
- **Internal or inbound PM**: Listed as a PM type or related role.
- **Adoption or customer success PM**: Listed as a PM type or related role.
- **"APM" has three meanings**: Mitchell's archetypes use APM for analytic PM, the coaching platform uses it for analytics PM, and everyone else uses it for associate PM. The coaching platform also uses DPM for digital, data and design PM in one article.
