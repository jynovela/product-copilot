# 9 product prioritization frameworks for product managers: framework extract

- Source: Tempo
- Date: not shown
- Pasted into the project by the user on 2026-11-20
- This file keeps only short paraphrases of what the article says about each framework. The article's own text is not copied here.

- **Opportunity scoring**: Comes from Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation; plot satisfaction against importance; respondents over- or understate importance.
- **RICE scoring**: Started as Intercom's internal system; doesn't account for dependencies; structured estimation, not prediction.
- **ICE scoring**: Mentioned as another model teams use.
- **Value vs effort matrix**: Fast initial triage; scoring debates expose strategy gaps; estimates are subjective and inconsistent at scale; pair with RICE afterwards.
- **Kano model**: Two axes, how fully a need is met and how satisfied customers are; uses a with-and-without questionnaire; needs a statistically meaningful sample.
- **MoSCoW method**: Should-haves are important but not time-sensitive and belong in the next release; teams overinflate must-haves; better for release criteria than ranking.
- **Cost of delay**: Joshua Arnold's definition; ask what a feature is worth now, how much more if earlier, and what each week of waiting costs; example: $30,000 vs $10,000 per week of delay with equal build time, so the first goes first.
- **Buy a feature**: Up to eight participants; price some features beyond one budget to force pooling; only evaluates features already on the roadmap.
- **Prune the product tree**: Developed by "Bruce Hollman"; trunk is existing features, outermost branches are the next release, inner branches are not yet on the roadmap; no quantitative output.
- **story-map**: One of the fastest ways to find the MVP; doesn't account for business value or technical complexity.
