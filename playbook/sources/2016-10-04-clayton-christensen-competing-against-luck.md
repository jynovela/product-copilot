## Summary

Christensen's theory of why customers buy, and how to innovate on it.
Chapters: 1 The milk shake dilemma, 2 Progress, not products, 3 Jobs in
the wild, 4 Job hunting, 5 How to hear what your customers don't say,
6 Building your résumé, 7 Integrating around a job, 8 Keeping your eye on the job,

## Key points

- A job is the progress a person seeks in a circumstance, with functional, emotional and social sides. See [[jobs-to-be-done]]
- Look for jobs in your own life, nonconsumption, workarounds, things people avoid, and odd uses. See [[finding-jobs]]
- Push and pull drive a switch; habit and anxiety block it; only repeat use proves the job is done. See [[forces-of-progress]]
- Rebuild a recent switch as a detailed story, then storyboard it. See [[jtbd-switch-interview]]
- A job spec lists dimensions, tradeoffs, competitors and obstacles; experiences, not features, get you hired. See [[job-spec]]
- Processes and metrics built around the job are the hard-to-copy advantage. See [[integrating-around-the-job]]
- Active data, surface growth and conforming data pull growing firms off the job. See [[innovation-data-fallacies]]

## Quotes

- "If you or a colleague describes a Job to Be Done in adjectives and adverbs, it is not a valid job." (ch10)
