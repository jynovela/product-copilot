## Summary

About 25 pieces on problem framing and sizing condensed into one file: problem statement formats and quality tests, root-cause and decomposition methods, ways to classify a problem (awareness ladder, painkiller vs vitamin, four types), sizing dimensions and ways to get the numbers, the opportunity canvas, TAM/SAM/SOM, top-down vs bottom-up, Fermi estimation, when to frame formally, estimation practice, 10 agreed practices and 8 conflicts.

Every claim in the raw file names the article it came from, and vendor claims, unsourced numbers and errors are flagged. Treat it as a map of what public sources say, not as fact, and never as evidence about this product's customers.

## Key points

- A problem statement never names a solution (section 1.1).
- Size by how many, how often and how badly, plus the cost to the user and to the business (practice 4).
- Prefer bottom-up sizing and aim for order of magnitude, not false precision (practices 8, 9).
- Sources disagree on whether to guess a number or leave it blank, and on scoring ideas in a spreadsheet (conflicts 1, 2).
- Only about ten of the pieces say anything specific about sizing (introduction).
