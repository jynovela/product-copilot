## Summary

About 47 pieces on experimentation, from product, vendor, consultancy, lab-science and peer-reviewed sources, condensed into one file: vocabulary, 20 end-to-end processes, hypothesis templates, a catalogue of experiment types from interviews to geo tests, matching tests to stage, when not to experiment, a statistics toolkit, a bias catalogue, what to do after a test, principle lists, 17 agreed practices and 14 conflicts. About 18 of the pieces come from vendors of experimentation tools.

Every claim in the raw file names the article it came from, and vendor claims, unsourced numbers and errors are flagged. Treat it as a map of what public sources say, not as fact, and never as evidence about this product's customers.

## Key points

- Write the hypothesis, metrics and success criteria before launch, and don't change them (practices 1, 2).
- One primary metric, a few guardrails and some secondary metrics (practice 3).
- Match the test to the stage: cheap pretotypes first, A/B tests when traffic allows (sections 2.3, 2.4).
- Peeking inflates false wins unless you chose sequential testing upfront (conflict 3).
- Sources disagree on what counts as an experiment, on qualitative vs A/B first, and on who decides after a test (conflicts 1, 2, 7).
