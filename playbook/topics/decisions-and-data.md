# Decisions and data

## What this covers

Cognitive biases, decision quality versus outcomes, statistics traps, and goal-setting with metrics.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis of the books below, from model memory.

- Judge decisions by the process and information available, not only by how they turned out.
- Our fast intuitive thinking is biased; slow down for important calls.
- Metrics can mislead; check samples, averages, charts and causation.

## Concepts

- [[resulting]]: Judging a decision by its outcome instead of by the decision process.
- [[premortem]]: Imagine the project failed, then explain why, before you start.
- [[innovation-data-fallacies]]: Active data, surface growth and conforming data pull firms off the job.
- [[system-1-and-system-2]]: Fast, intuitive thinking versus slow, deliberate thinking, and the biases that come from the first.
- [[statistical-traps]]: Common ways numbers mislead: biased samples, chosen averages, misleading charts, false causation.
- [[okrs]]: A goal (objective) plus a few measurable results that show progress toward it.
- [[wrap-decision-process]]: Widen options, Reality-test assumptions, Attain distance, Prepare to be wrong.
- [[theory-of-constraints]]: A system's output is limited by its bottleneck; improve the bottleneck first.
- [[five-whys]]: Ask why a problem happened, then why that happened, about five times, to reach a root cause.
- [[first-principles-thinking]]: Break a problem down to what you know is true, then reason up from there instead of by analogy.
- [[opportunity-scoring]]: Rank customer outcomes by how important they are and how poorly they are served today.
- [[rice-scoring]]: Score ideas by reach x impact x confidence / effort to rank them against one goal.
- [[ice-scoring]]: A quick score of impact, confidence and ease for ranking growth experiments.
- [[value-vs-effort-matrix]]: A 2x2 of value against effort to spot quick wins and drop low-value, high-effort work.
- [[weighted-scoring]]: Score each idea against several weighted criteria, such as revenue, strategic fit and effort, and rank by total.
- [[kano-model]]: Sort features by how they affect satisfaction: basic expectations, performance features and delighters.
- [[moscow-method]]: Sort the scope of a release or timebox into must, should, could and won't have this time.
- [[cost-of-delay]]: Put a value on what each week of waiting costs, and use it to order work.
- [[daci-and-raci]]: Name who drives, who decides, who contributes and who is informed, so decisions don't stall.
- [[north-star-framework]]: One metric that best captures the value customers get, plus the input metrics teams can move to drive it.
- [[aarrr-pirate-metrics]]: Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue: Dave McClure's funnel of startup metrics.
- [[heart-framework]]: Google's user-experience metrics: happiness, engagement, adoption, retention, task success.
- [[rapid-decision-roles]]: Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide; one Decider.
- [[spade-framework]]: Setting, People, Alternatives, Decide, Explain for hard, fast calls.
- [[one-way-and-two-way-doors]]: Irreversible decisions need care; reversible ones need speed.
- [[decision-matrix]]: Weighted criteria with a sensitivity check.
- [[decision-rules]]: Agree who decides and how you'll know it's decided.
- [[kill-criteria]]: Signals set in advance that a bet isn't working.
- [[vroom-yetton-jago]]: Choose how much to involve the team.
- [[ooda-loop]]: Observe, orient, decide, act, repeat.
- [[goodharts-law]]: Why targets get gamed.
- [[issue-tree]]: Break a problem into MECE causes.

## What the research digest adds

From about 37 pieces on decision making (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]). Section numbers point into that page's raw file.

Decision processes (1.1): the seven-step process (identify, gather, alternatives, weigh, choose, act, review) with Atlassian's tool and trap for each step; Naik's six-step product cycle that always lists do nothing, tweak and build new; Cheesecake Labs' ten steps; SPADE; Bain's What, Who, How, When and five organizational steps with decision architecture; Torres's outcome-based process; IBM's data-driven steps and four kinds of analysis; Walker's PreAmble and classification grid (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Who decides (1.2): RAPID, DACI, RACI, Pichler's four decision rules, MIT's five team models with a consensus check, Vroom-Yetton-Jago, Stewart's three styles, and group methods that limit dominant voices (nominal group, Delphi, brainwriting, multivoting) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Classifying decisions (1.3): Type 1 and Type 2; satisficing vs maximizing; strategic vs operational; Bain's critical decisions (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Evaluating options (1.4): decision matrix (unweighted, weighted, Pugh); decision tree; Annie Duke's preferences, payoffs and probabilities; pros and cons, cost-benefit, SWOT, PEST, Pareto, conjoint, RICE, MoSCoW, Eisenhower, effort vs impact, six thinking hats, logic model, root cause analysis, trade-off analysis; game theory for pricing moves; futurizing 3, 5 and 10 years out; fear setting; the ladder of inference; sorting facts from assumptions; scientist mode. The "37% rule" as posted is a loose version of optimal stopping; don't use it as stated (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Risk and revisiting (1.5): premortem, kill criteria, Rekhi's "what new information?" test before reopening, OODA, recognition-primed decisions, speed tactics (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

AI (1.6): use AI as a co-thinker to widen options, test them against criteria and list risks, not to make the call (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Biases named: confirmation, anchoring, availability, representativeness, sunk cost, groupthink, status quo, authority, halo and horns, professional deformation, hindsight, illusory truth, bias blind spot, overconfidence, extrapolation, framing (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Same word, different meaning: DACI's Approver makes the decision, SPADE's Approver only vetoes the process; RACI's Responsible does the work, SPADE's Responsible decides; RAPID's Agree is a sign-off, not the decision (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Practices most sources agree on: define the decision before debating options; generate more than two real options, including doing nothing; make clear who decides and how, beforehand; match the process to the decision; get diverse input and limit dominant voices; communicate the decision and the why; watch for named biases; review decisions afterwards; set deadlines (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

Contradictions:

- One decider (RAPID, SPADE) vs group rules (Pichler, MIT, Atlassian) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).
- Is the PM the decider: yes (Rekhi), one option of four (Pichler), or the PM drives others to decide (MohammedBhai) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).
- Speed vs deliberation (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).
- Reopening: high hurdle (Bain) vs few decisions are permanent (PM-2025, Chakraborty) vs reopen on new information (Rekhi) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).
- Data vs intuition: data instead of gut (IBM, Momentum) vs intuition as a hypothesis to test (Chakraborty) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).
- Multi-criteria scoring vs ranking on one criterion (Torres) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]]).

## Questions to ask about your product

- What would make this decision wrong?
- What's the sample behind this metric?
- Are we judging the last launch by luck or by decision quality?
- Which recent decisions have a decision page naming who decided, the options considered and when to revisit?
- Was this decision a one-way or a two-way door, and did the process match?
