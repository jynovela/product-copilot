# Positioning and go-to-market

## What this covers

Positioning, category choice, adoption across customer segments, and messages that stick.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis of the books below, from model memory.

- Positioning lives in the customer's mind; own a clear idea or category.
- Win a narrow beachhead segment completely before expanding to pragmatic buyers.
- Messages stick when they're simple, concrete and told as stories.

## What the research digest adds

From about 16 web pieces on go-to-market, all from vendors, agencies, research firms or course sellers (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]). Section numbers point into that page's raw file.

- Definitions, and how GTM differs from marketing strategy, a marketing plan, product strategy, a business plan and a launch plan (section 1) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- When a GTM plan is needed: Product School's threshold is a bet that changes who you serve, how you sell or how you make money, not every UI tweak (section 1) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Component lists: PMA's five elements (market, customers, distribution, messaging and positioning, price), the 4 Ps, and lists from Pragmatic, Dovetail, Product School, Salesforce, AEVO and Info-Tech (2.1 to 2.3) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Process sequences: PMA's 7 steps, Product School's 8, Info-Tech's three phases and 17 steps with proceed, pivot or stop gates, Kaizen, Hinge, Coursera, Salesforce, Tripp, Wrike, Pragmatic (2.4) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Motions and growth models: funnel, flywheel, product-led, account-based, sales-led, partner-led, hybrid (2.5) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Channels: concentrate where the ICP looks; give each channel a hypothesis, owner and measure; Kaizen dissents in favour of many channels (2.6) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Buying center roles (2.7); rollout waves (2.8) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Positioning, value proposition and messaging: positioning before messaging; Kaizen's competitive advantage, money value and USP exercises; proof points; message house; customer as hero; don't lead with "AI-powered" (2.9) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Competitive analysis for GTM: known competitors vs competitive alternatives, substitutes and non-consumption, win/loss analysis (2.10) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Pricing, sales enablement, team and governance inside GTM (2.11 to 2.13) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Success metrics: metric, amount and timeframe; outcomes not activity; Product School's metric families and three horizons (2.14) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- B2B vs B2C differences; adoption after launch; AI in GTM (2.15 to 2.17) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).

Practices most sources agree on: start GTM during development; pick a narrow target first; ground positioning in research; differentiate against named alternatives including doing nothing; align product, marketing and sales on one story; name an owner; enable sales before launch; set measurable success criteria before launch; validate with pilots or limited releases; treat GTM as a loop (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).

Contradictions:

- Who owns GTM: product marketing (PMA, Aventi, Dovetail), RevOps and sales (Salesforce), shared (Pragmatic), or depends on stage (Product School) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- One plan per launch (PMA, Dovetail, Wrike) vs a lasting operating system (AEVO, Info-Tech, Product School) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Market sizing in GTM (Info-Tech, Kaizen, Tripp) vs not (Product School) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Few channels vs many at once (Kaizen) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Success as sales efficiency (Coursera, Salesforce) vs activation first (Product School, Pragmatic) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).

Gaps: no independent practitioners such as April Dunford or Geoffrey Moore, no definition of launch tiers, and no real launch brief or post-launch review (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).

## Questions to ask about your product

- What single idea do we want to own in customers' minds?
- Which segment is our beachhead?
- Who do customers compare us to?
- What is our go-to-market motion, and does it match our price point and how customers buy?
- Who is in the buying center for our main segment, and what does each role need to hear?
