# Strategy and innovation

## What this covers

Product vision and strategy, how innovation happens, and why good companies miss disruptive shifts.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis of the books below, from model memory.

- Strategy is focus: choosing the few problems and customers that matter most.
- Disruption starts small and "worse" in overlooked markets; incumbents ignore it rationally.
- Success is measured in outcomes for customers and the business, not features shipped.

## What the research digest adds

From about 31 articles on product strategy (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]). Section numbers point into that page's raw file.

Frameworks and models:

- Perri's product strategy canvas and the Product Kata (1.1); Pichler's elements of a strategy, Product Vision Board, four-level framework, Strategy Stack and life-cycle inflection points (1.2 to 1.5) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Ant Murphy's four steps: inputs, strategic narratives, leverage, choices; a scaffold for each choice (1.6) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Hustle Badger's strategy stack and seven steps: objective, users, superpowers, vision, 2 to 4 pillars, impact model, roadmap (1.7) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Helmer's 7 Powers (1.8); where to play and how to win (1.9); strategy as insight raised to conviction (1.10) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- An anonymous three-year process: research, pain-point hypotheses, the biggest problem worth solving, a daily North Star, 3 to 5 levers (1.12) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Hemnet's four principles: at least a year, explicit, co-created, directions not constraints (1.13) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Strategy by company stage: product-market fit, scaling, optimization (1.14) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Company vs product strategy, and common confusions: an objective, a roadmap or tactics presented as strategy (1.15) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Des Traynor's twelve arguments that sneak features in, and why saying no is the job (1.16) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Strategy-type lists that don't agree: Ansoff-style growth directions, Porter-style types, a ten-type list, Urban and Hauser's nine, Productboard's development types, Dragonboat's table (1.17) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Triggers to rethink a strategy: falling sales or renewals, shifting needs, new technology, new entrants, a saturated market (1.19) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- What vendor templates put in a strategy document (1.20); technical defensibility and the "would an architect get requirements from it?" test (1.21) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).

Practices most sources agree on: strategy is choices and focus; start from customer needs; name an advantage competitors can't easily copy; tie it to business goals; the roadmap comes after; review at least quarterly starting from assumptions; treat a new strategy as a hypothesis; co-create and communicate it; be explicit; adapt to the stage (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).

Contradictions:

- Measurable goals inside the strategy (Perri, Hustle Badger, ProdPad) vs inside but unmeasured (Pichler) vs separate (Murphy) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Fixed (Railsware) vs living (airfocus, ProdPad, Pichler's continuous strategizing) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Horizon: 12 to 24 months (Pichler) vs at least a year, ideally three (Hemnet) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Features in the strategy: 3 to 5 standout features (Pichler) vs never features (Hemnet, Perri) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Go-to-market and roadmap inside the strategy document or not (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Mission vs vision: the words mean opposite things in different sources (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Responding to customer requests listed as a strategy vs the saying-no sources (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).
- Errors to avoid repeating: *Playing to Win* is by Lafley and Martin, not Martin alone; DHM is Delight, Hard to copy, Margin-enhancing; Salesforce, not Microsoft, bought Slack (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]]).

## Questions to ask about your product

- Which low-end or non-obvious alternatives are customers using?
- What outcome does the roadmap serve?
- Where are we overshooting what customers need?
- Is there an active strategy page with at least a challenge and where to play? If not, the overview should say "No strategy recorded".
- When was the strategy last reviewed, and which of its assumptions has new evidence?
