# Goals and metrics

## What this covers

How to pick metrics that show whether a product or feature is working, and how to write goals and OKRs around them: metric types, North Star and funnel frameworks, how metrics get gamed, goal checklists and hierarchies, OKR writing, scoring and traps. It condenses about 39 articles on success metrics and about 75 on goals and OKRs; the full detail is in [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]] and [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]].

In this wiki, each metric is defined once on a metric page, goals are goal pages with a level and a commitment, and the word is "metric" or "target", never "KPI".

## Core ideas

- Start from the goal, then choose metrics; "if you don't know what you are trying to accomplish, it is too early for KPIs" (BSI) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Measure value, not just usage; judge a feature by one primary metric and protect the rest with guardrails (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Any measure that becomes a target gets gamed; diversify, pre-game and pair growth metrics with guardrails (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Goals describe outcomes, not outputs, and state a baseline, a target and a date (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Committed goals should be hit; aspirational goals are expected to land near 70% (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Goals and OKRs are not a strategy and can't replace one (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).

## Practices most sources agree on

Metrics (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]):

1. Start from the goal, then choose metrics.
2. Keep to a few metrics.
3. Balance leading and lagging.
4. Avoid vanity metrics; measure outcomes, not activity.
5. Make metrics actionable.
6. Define each metric once and keep the calculation consistent; note changes.
7. Get a baseline before setting a target.
8. Give each metric an owner.
9. Review on a schedule and retire stale metrics.
10. Pair numbers with qualitative evidence.
11. Watch for gaming.
12. Segment; don't trust averages.
13. Mind data quality.
14. Tell the story behind the numbers.

Goals (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]):

1. Outcomes, not outputs.
2. Few goals.
3. Baseline, target and date.
4. One owner per goal or key result.
5. Ambitious but believable.
6. Check in during the cycle.
7. Connect every team goal to a higher goal or strategy.
8. Mix top-down and bottom-up.
9. Strategy comes first.
10. Keep goals out of individual performance ratings.
11. Pair growth metrics with guardrails.
12. Prefer leading indicators the team can move.
13. Goals needn't cover all work.
14. Change goals only deliberately.
15. Review and learn at the end of each cycle.

## Contradictions

- One number vs many: a single best metric or North Star vs diversify because any one metric gets gamed; the usual resolution is one primary plus counter-metrics (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- When to set the target: before launch (Siegel, Asana, Product School) vs only after watching the metric a while (Giannakopoulos) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Tie targets to rewards: yes (NetSuite, 12 Characteristics) vs no (Rego, LSA, Manheim) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Benchmarks as targets: match the industry vs use your own baseline (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- What a KPI is: seven incompatible definitions (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Is adoption a success metric: yes for several vendors; only secondary for Siegel (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- Direct metric vs link to business metrics: only direct metrics count (Miki) vs tie every feature up the chain (Stukan, Alwan, Gupta); compatible if the direct metric decides (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]).
- How many goals: one, 1 to 3, or 3 to 5 per team (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- How many key results: about 3, 2 to 4, 2 to 5, 3 to 5 or up to 5 (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- What hit rate means success: 0.6 to 0.7 for aspirational, 1.0 for committed, about 50%, or 80 to 90% (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Outcome-only vs outputs allowed (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Cascading: strict cascade vs Herbig's "weak links, not strong ties" (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- OKRs in performance reviews: keep out (most) vs include (Toast) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Cycle length: a quarter is the norm; 6 months for later-stage companies (BCV); shorter in discovery (Herbig) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Use OKRs in product management at all: yes for most; Pichler is "not a fan" (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).

## Numbers to treat with care

Benchmarks such as "LTV at least 3× CAC", "NPS above 50 is excellent", "churn 2 to 8% is typical" and "stickiness above 20% is good" are unsourced or vendor claims; use them as context, never as a target without a primary source. Several vendor formulas are wrong (HubSpot's annual churn, Dovetail's MRR growth and NPS scale). Goal statistics such as "42% more likely to achieve written goals" are misquoted versions of one small study (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]], [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).

## Questions to ask about your product

- Does every active goal page state a baseline, a target, a date, an owner and whether it is committed or aspirational?
- Is any goal written as an output ("launch X")? Rewrite it as the behaviour change it should cause.
- Does each feature's primary metric measure value, and does it have a guardrail?
- Is each metric defined once on a metric page, with a dated note for every definition change?
