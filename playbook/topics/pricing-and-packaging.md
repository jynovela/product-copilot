# Pricing and packaging

## What this covers

How products are priced: the strategies that set the price level, the models and value metrics customers see, how to research what people will pay, and how to change prices. It condenses about 40 articles; the full detail, with every source named, is in [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]].

This is general craft. In this wiki, plans and prices live as a table in the overview, and every price change is a decision page that names who decided.

## Core ideas

- Cost sets the floor, what the customer values sets the ceiling, and competitors' prices sit in between and shape what feels fair (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- The strategy sets the level; the model is the format the customer pays in; the value metric is the unit you charge by (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Software sources call value-based pricing the default for B2B SaaS; cost-plus is called the most common strategy overall (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Pricing is a team effort; most sources say the PM rarely owns the number alone (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Most sources are vendors or course sellers, and several get basic definitions wrong; the concept pages note where.

## Practices most sources agree on

1. Start from the business goal: share or profit (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
2. Know your full cost; it is the floor (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
3. Research what customers value and will pay, by segment (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
4. Benchmark competitors, including their price history, but don't just copy (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
5. Combine strategies; few firms use one alone (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
6. Communicate value before or with the price (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
7. Test and keep reviewing (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
8. Treat pricing as a team effort (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
9. Add value before discounting (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
10. Change prices carefully: grandfather, explain, align sales first (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
11. Shift strategy across the product life cycle (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).

## Contradictions

- Best willingness-to-pay method: A/B tests (Gupta) vs conjoint (Qualtrics, Sawtooth, both of which sell it) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Per-seat pricing: a "relic" (Paddle) vs standard with AI as an add-on (ProdPad) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Good-better-best: "dying out" in software (Paddle) vs a staple (Gupta, Shopify, Product School, Qualtrics) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Which tier buyers pick: the cheapest (ProductPlan) vs the middle (Product School, Qualtrics) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Review cadence: every 6 months, quarterly, at least yearly, on triggers, or stick with it once set (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Does the PM own the number: Gupta yes; Aha!, ProductPlan, Product School, Olafioye no (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Is freemium pricing: an acquisition model (Paddle) vs a pricing model (most) (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Most common strategy: cost-plus overall vs value-based in SaaS; Inference: both hold in their own markets (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Charge more or less: Peep Laja says more; penetration advocates start low (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Single currency vs localization: Peep Laja thinks in USD; Paddle, PMA and Shopify localize (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- How many strategies exist: lists of 3, 4, 5, 7 and 10 with different members; there is no canonical list (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).
- Errors to avoid repeating: Shopify and Aha! confuse markup with margin; Peep Laja swaps the Economist decoy prices; Indeed misdefines skimming (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).

## Numbers to treat with care

Vendor claims such as "small pricing changes move revenue 20 to 50%", "pricing is 7.5x more powerful than acquisition", "discounts over 20% correlate with churn" and "the right currency symbol adds 30%" come from Paddle and aren't independently checked. The full flagged table is in section 5 of the digest (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]]).

## Questions to ask about your product

- What is our value metric, and does it grow with the value customers get? Check the overview's pricing table.
- Which segments do we price for, and what evidence of willingness to pay do we have for each? Look for customer pages with a Willingness to pay line.
- Who decided our current prices, and is there a decision page for each change?
- What do competitors charge, and how has that changed? Check the dated Pricing lines on competitor pages.
