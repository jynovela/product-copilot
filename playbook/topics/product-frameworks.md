# Product frameworks

## What this covers

The common PM frameworks, grouped by the job they do, with combinations that work and notes on unreliable sources. Each framework has its own concept page.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis from model memory, checked against the framework articles cited on each concept page.

- A framework makes trade-offs visible and gives a group shared terms; it doesn't make the decision.
- Pick the framework for the job and the data you have. Scoring models need estimates; survey models need customers; workshop games need a room.
- Combine them: a fast triage first, a careful method on what survives.
- Most framework articles are vendor content. They simplify, and some get the framework wrong; check the concept page's Contradictions section.

## Frameworks by job

### Understand customers and problems

- [[jobs-to-be-done]]: Define what customers hire a product for as progress in a specific circumstance, so you see the real competition and what to build.
- [[mom-test-interviews]]: Ask about people's real past behavior, not their opinion of your idea.
- [[customer-journey-map]]: A visual of every step a customer takes with your product, with what they do, think and feel at each.
- [[opportunity-scoring]]: Rank customer needs by how important they are and how poorly they are served today, using Olsen's importance versus satisfaction quadrants and value formulas or Ulwick's opportunity score.
- [[five-whys]]: Ask why a problem happened, then why that happened, about five times, to reach a root cause.
- [[first-principles-thinking]]: Break a problem down to what you know is true, then reason up from there instead of by analogy.
- [[fogg-behavior-model]]: A behavior happens when motivation, ability and a prompt come together at the same moment (B=MAP).
- [[value-proposition-canvas]]: Match a customer profile (jobs, pains, gains) to a value map (products, pain relievers, gain creators).

### Explore and test solutions

- [[design-thinking]]: A human-centered way to solve problems: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test.
- [[double-diamond]]: Discover, define, develop, deliver: diverge then converge on the problem, then on the solution.
- [[design-sprint]]: A five-day process to answer a big question with a prototype and five customer tests.
- [[opportunity-solution-tree]]: A visual map from a desired outcome to customer opportunities, solutions and assumption tests.
- [[build-measure-learn]]: Ship a minimum viable product, measure what happens, learn, and repeat fast.
- [[minimum-viable-product]]: The smallest thing you can build to test a key assumption and learn, not just a small first release.
- [[working-backwards-pr-faq]]: Start a product by writing the press release and FAQ from the customer's point of view.
- [[circles-method]]: A structure for product design interview answers.
- [[mvp-test-matrix]]: Choose how to test an MVP from a 2x2 of product versus marketing and qualitative versus quantitative tests, from five-second tests and landing pages to clickable mockups, concierge MVPs and fake doors.
- [[product-kata]]: Reach an outcome goal with Perri's Product Kata: six questions from goal to learning, then problem exploration, build-to-learn solution experiments with a pass-or-fail ask, and building until the goal is met.

### Prioritize and scope

- [[value-vs-effort-matrix]]: A 2x2 of value against effort to spot quick wins and drop low-value, high-effort work.
- [[rice-scoring]]: Score ideas by reach x impact x confidence / effort to rank them against one goal.
- [[ice-scoring]]: A quick score of impact, confidence and ease for ranking growth experiments.
- [[weighted-scoring]]: Score each idea against several weighted criteria, such as revenue, strategic fit and effort, and rank by total.
- [[kano-model]]: Sort features by how they affect satisfaction: basic expectations, performance features and delighters.
- [[moscow-method]]: Sort the scope of a release or timebox into must, should, could and won't have this time.
- [[cost-of-delay]]: Put a value on what each week of waiting costs, and use it to order work.
- [[buy-a-feature]]: A game where customers spend play money on priced features, showing what they value most.
- [[prune-the-product-tree]]: A workshop game where customers place feature ideas on a drawn tree to show where to grow the product.
- [[story-map]]: Lay out the user's journey as a backbone of activities with stories beneath, then slice releases.
- [[eisenhower-matrix]]: Sort tasks by urgent and important: do, schedule, delegate or drop.
- [[mvp-feature-set]]: Choose an MVP candidate: brainstorm features per benefit, write them as user stories, break them into small chunks, rank by value over effort, and pick the chunks that make the product viable and different.

### Set strategy and goals

- [[kernel-of-good-strategy]]: Diagnosis, guiding policy and coherent actions: Rumelt's core of any real strategy.
- [[swot-analysis]]: List strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats to assess a move.
- [[five-forces]]: Industry profit is shaped by rivalry, buyers, suppliers, substitutes and new entrants; advantage comes from being different.
- [[ansoff-matrix]]: Four growth options: sell more to current markets, new products, new markets, or both new.
- [[business-model-canvas]]: Nine blocks on one page that describe how a business creates, delivers and captures value.
- [[product-life-cycle]]: Products move through introduction, growth, maturity and decline, and each stage needs a different strategy.
- [[technology-adoption-lifecycle]]: Adoption moves from innovators to laggards, with a chasm between early adopters and the early majority.
- [[okrs]]: A goal (objective) plus a few measurable results that show progress toward it.
- [[impact-mapping]]: Map a goal to the actors who can affect it, the behavior changes needed, and only then the deliverables.
- [[gist-planning]]: Goals, ideas, step-projects, tasks: Itamar Gilad's lighter alternative to detailed roadmaps.
- [[value-proposition-grid]]: A table of must-haves, performance benefits and delighters scored for you and each competitor, now and later, that states where you'll win and what you'll leave alone.
- [[strategy-deployment]]: Turn company strategy into direction teams can act on: close Perri's knowledge, alignment and effects gaps by cascading vision, strategic intents, product initiatives and options, reviewed on a fixed cadence.

### Measure

- [[north-star-framework]]: One metric that best captures the value customers get, plus the input metrics teams can move to drive it.
- [[aarrr-pirate-metrics]]: Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue: Dave McClure's funnel of startup metrics.
- [[heart-framework]]: Google's user-experience metrics: happiness, engagement, adoption, retention, task success.
- [[hook-model]]: Trigger, action, variable reward, investment: the loop behind habit-forming products.
- [[metric-that-matters-most]]: After launch, pick the one AARRR metric with the best return right now (usually retention, then conversion, then acquisition) and improve it with Olsen's Lean Product Analytics Process.

### Decide and organize the team

- [[daci-and-raci]]: Name who drives, who decides, who contributes and who is informed, so decisions don't stall.
- [[wrap-decision-process]]: Widen options, Reality-test assumptions, Attain distance, Prepare to be wrong.
- [[dual-track-agile]]: Run discovery (what to build) and delivery (building it) in parallel, with the same team.
- [[agile-and-scrum]]: Deliver in short iterations with feedback; Scrum is the most common way to run it.
- [[stage-gate]]: Split development into stages with go/kill decision gates between them, to control risk on costly products.
- [[spotify-model]]: Spotify's 2012 picture of autonomous squads grouped into tribes, with chapters and guilds across them.
- [[team-topologies-model]]: Four team types, three interaction modes, and team design around cognitive load and Conway's law.

## Combinations that work

Inference: these pairings are my suggestions, not from a source.

- Discovery loop: [[jobs-to-be-done]] interviews (asked the [[mom-test-interviews]] way) feed an [[opportunity-solution-tree]]; size opportunities with [[opportunity-scoring]]; test solutions with a [[minimum-viable-product]] inside [[build-measure-learn]]; run it as [[dual-track-agile]].
- Quarterly planning: pick a [[north-star-framework]] metric and set [[okrs]] on its inputs; triage ideas with a [[value-vs-effort-matrix]], then rank the survivors with [[rice-scoring]] against the OKR; use [[cost-of-delay]] when timing matters.
- Release scoping: lay out a [[story-map]], cut the first slice, then agree it with stakeholders using [[moscow-method]] and check the basics with the [[kano-model]].
- New product bet: write the [[working-backwards-pr-faq]], frame the business on a [[business-model-canvas]], diagnose with the [[kernel-of-good-strategy]] (use [[swot-analysis]] and [[five-forces]] as inputs) and place the bet on the [[ansoff-matrix]].
- Growth: find the leakiest [[aarrr-pirate-metrics]] stage, use the [[fogg-behavior-model]] to see why users stall, and rank experiments with [[ice-scoring]].
- Decision hygiene: name roles with [[daci-and-raci]], then use the [[wrap-decision-process]] and a [[premortem]].

## Other frameworks in the sources

Listed in the articles but not given their own page, because they are minor, narrow, or not really PM frameworks.

- **Affinity grouping**: Group ideas into themes, then vote within each theme. A workshop technique more than a framework. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **Eisenhower's 4 Ds (do, defer, delegate, dump)**: Four ways to handle any task; see [[eisenhower-matrix]]. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **Quality Function Deployment (QFD)**: Translates customer needs into technical requirements, usually with a 'house of quality' matrix. Common in manufacturing. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **The User Is Drunk**: A usability test: could someone drunk use it? A memorable push toward simplicity, not a method. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **KEY design process**: A two-part design process (opportunity, then solution) the article attributes to UXPlanet. I don't know it well. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **Kennedy principle**: Don't make users do work the product could do for them, such as typing a state after a ZIP code. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **Classical vs behavioral economics**: Two assumptions about customers: rational actors or biased ones. See [[choice-architecture]] and [[system-1-and-system-2]]. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **Agile methods list: Crystal, Disciplined Agile, DSDM, eXtreme Programming, Feature-Driven Development, Rapid Application Development, SAFe, Lean Software Development**: Delivery methods for engineering teams. Summarized on [[agile-and-scrum]]. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- **3 Pillars of Product and Product Team Competencies**: Neal Cabage's own frameworks: discovery, planning and development as the three pillars; and a strategic-tactical, external-internal grid of PM skills. The author's own models, so not independent. (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]])
- **Product development stages (six stages)**: Ideation, definition, prototyping, initial design, validation, commercialization. A linear, physical-product view; compare [[stage-gate]] and [[dual-track-agile]]. (see [[product-development-process-guide]])
- **Waterfall**: Sequential phases, each finished before the next. See [[agile-and-scrum]]. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])
- **Storytelling**: A narrative structure (hero, problem, transformation) for presenting a product. See [[communication-and-storytelling]]. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])
- **Sales objectives**: Listed as a PM framework, but it is goal setting for sales, not a PM framework. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])

## Weak or wrongly described entries

- **RFM (recency, frequency, monetary value)**: Called "the most popular product framework" for prioritizing product development, similar to Kano. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]) General knowledge: RFM is a customer segmentation method from direct marketing. It scores customers on how recently, how often and how much they buy. It doesn't prioritize features and isn't related to Kano.
- **REAN**: Described as Reduce, Elevate, Amplify, Nurture, "three essential parts" that are then listed as four. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]) General knowledge: REAN is usually Reach, Engage, Activate, Nurture, a digital marketing measurement model. Confidence: medium.
- **Growth-Driven Design**: Described as "also known as A/B testing or split testing". (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]) General knowledge: Growth-Driven Design is HubSpot's method for building websites in small, continuous iterations instead of big redesigns. It uses tests, but it isn't another name for A/B testing.
- **AIDA(R)**: A marketing funnel: attention, interest, desire, action, retention. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]) General knowledge: a decades-old advertising model. Fine for messaging; [[aarrr-pirate-metrics]] fits product funnels better.
- **5 Es**: One source calls journey maps "sometimes called the 5 Es" (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]]); another describes a marketing campaign method: engage, explore, express, exchange, extend (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: the best-known 5 Es are an experience model (entice, enter, engage, exit, extend). The two sources use the same name for different things.

## Questions to ask about your product

- Which decision is this framework helping us make, and who decides?
- Do we have the inputs it needs, or are we guessing?
- Are we using one scoring convention for every item?
