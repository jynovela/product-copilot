# Types of product managers

## What this covers

The PM titles and specializations that job ads and articles use, grouped by what actually differs between them, with notes on where the articles contradict each other. Salary and compensation claims in the sources were left out on purpose.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis from model memory, checked against the articles cited on each concept page.

- There is no standard list of PM types. Every source slices the role differently, and the slices overlap.
- Level (associate to CPO) and type (growth, technical, platform) are separate axes. Several sources mix them in one list.
- A type names the part of the job that matters most in that role. Every PM still does discovery, prioritization and delivery.
- The same title means different things at different companies. Read the responsibilities, not the title.
- Most articles here are vendor, training-company or recruiter content. They simplify, copy each other, and sometimes get roles wrong.

## Types by axis

### By level

- [[pm-career-ladder]]: The levels of a PM career, from associate PM to CPO, and the split between the people-manager and individual-contributor tracks.

### By technical depth

- [[technical-product-manager]]: A PM on a technically complex product who works closer to engineering on architecture, APIs and trade-offs.
- [[data-product-manager]]: A PM for data as a product: pipelines, analytics, data platforms, or features built on data. Sources define it very differently.
- [[ai-product-manager]]: A PM for products or features built on machine learning or LLMs, who manages uncertain model behavior, data and ethics.

### By customer

- [[enterprise-vs-consumer-pm]]: How the PM job changes when you sell to businesses (buyers differ from users, long sales cycles) or to individuals (scale, engagement).
- [[platform-product-manager]]: A PM whose customers are other teams or developers: shared services, internal tools, APIs and infrastructure.

### By goal and stage

- [[growth-product-manager]]: A PM who owns a business metric such as activation, retention or revenue and moves it through experiments.
- [[types-of-product-work]]: Zero-to-one, then feature, growth, scaling and PMF-expansion work: each needs different processes, metrics and PMs.

### By the person

- [[pm-archetypes]]: Person-based patterns of PM strengths (growth, UX-inclined, technician, white-spacer and others) used to match people to work.
- [[generalist-vs-specialist-pm]]: Whether a PM should cover the whole job or specialize (growth, technical, data, AI), and when each pays off.

### Roles next to PM

- [[product-owner-vs-product-manager]]: The Scrum product owner manages the team's backlog; a product manager owns the product's outcome. Sometimes one person, sometimes two roles.
- [[product-operations]]: A function that makes product teams more effective through data, tools, processes and customer insight. Sources split on what it is.
- [[product-marketing-manager]]: The role that owns positioning, messaging, launch and go-to-market. A close partner of the PM, usually not a PM.

## How to use these types

Inference: my suggestions, drawing on the concept pages.

- Read a job ad on three axes: level, customer (business, consumer, internal) and main risk (technical, growth, data, AI). The title alone rarely tells you.
- Match the type of work to the person: zero-to-one needs someone comfortable with ambiguity, scaling needs systems thinking, growth needs experiment discipline.
- When a source calls a role a PM type, check whether it owns product decisions. Product owner, product ops and PMM are usually partners, not PMs.
- Expect titles to mean different things at different companies, especially enterprise PM, data PM, senior PM and product lead.

## Other types in the sources

Named in the articles but not given their own page, because they are narrow, an industry label, or not really PM roles.

- **UX or design PM**: Owns the user experience and works closely with designers and researchers; suits designers moving into PM. Product School's version is a design manager who leads design teams, which is a design role. (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]], [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]], [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]], [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- **Digital PM**: Manages digital products such as software, apps and online services. ProdPad says the title is now redundant because most PM work is digital; Product School treats it as a distinct role. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]], [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- **Software PM**: Manages software from conception to retirement; mostly an older or industry-specific title for a PM. (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]], [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- **Mobile PM**: Specializes in mobile apps: app stores, SDKs, mobile UX and app metrics. (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[online-school-types-of-product-manager]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- **Hardware PM**: Manages physical products with longer cycles, manufacturing, supply chains and vendor partners. (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]], [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- **Domain or industry PM**: Builds on years in one domain (such as martech) or industry (such as healthcare, finance or government), including its business processes. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- **Fintech PM**: Works on payments, banking or blockchain products, with regulation, risk and security. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- **Internal or inbound PM**: Builds tools used inside the company; stresses stakeholder work and saying no. See [[platform-product-manager]]. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-03-05-adam-fishman-pm-archetypes]])
- **Adoption or customer success PM**: Makes sure customers actually use what they bought, through training, content and enablement; found at large companies. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- **Ecosystem or partner PM**: Manages partner relationships and third-party integrations. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])
- **API PM**: Builds API platforms for developer communities. See [[platform-product-manager]]. (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- **E-commerce PM**: Improves online shopping and sales performance. (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- **Innovation or product strategy manager**: Defines long-term vision and strategy. ProdPad calls product strategist an adjacent role. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]], [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- **Agile PM**: A PM who works in sprints with constant iteration; inference: this describes a way of working that most PMs share, not a type. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- **Freelance PM**: A contract PM brought in for a defined project or gap. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- **Startup PM**: Covers everything, often as the only PM. See [[types-of-product-work]]. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]], [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]], [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- **Product analyst**: Analyzes usage data to inform product decisions; product-adjacent. (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- **Product specialist**: The expert on specific features who supports sales, success or training; product-adjacent. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- **Analytics PM**: Studies user behavior with analytics tools to find improvements. (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- **Sustainability and Web3 PMs**: Named as emerging specializations without detail. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- **Function-specific PMs (IoT, SaaS)**: Named as examples of PMs focused on one kind of product. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])

## Weak or wrongly described entries

- **"APM" has three meanings**: Mitchell's archetypes use APM for analytic PM (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]]), the coaching platform uses it for analytics PM (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]]), and everyone else uses it for associate PM. The coaching platform also uses DPM for digital, data and design PM in one article (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]]). Spell out the title.
- **UX/product design manager as a PM type**: Product School's entry describes someone who leads design teams and sets UX standards (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]]). General knowledge: that is a design management role, a partner to PMs, not a type of PM.
- **UX PMs create wireframes and prototypes**: HelloPM (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]]). General knowledge: PMs may sketch, but wireframes and prototypes are normally design work.
- **Other job titles for PMM and consumer PM**: Ally Mexicotte gives the same list (acquisition, activation, retention, pricing PM and so on) for growth, consumer and PMM roles (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]]). Inference: likely a copy-paste error; those are growth titles.
- **Product management dates from the 1940s**: Ally Mexicotte (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]]). General knowledge: the role is usually traced to Neil McElroy's 1931 brand-manager memo at Procter & Gamble. Confidence: medium.
- **Three types of product management**: JustAnotherPM's FAQ says they are platform, growth and innovation (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]]). No basis is given, and the same article lists five different types.
- **Degree requirements**: Product Faculty says data PMs need an MA in data management and PMMs and growth PMs an MA in marketing (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]]). General knowledge: these are not typical requirements; most PM roles ask for experience, not a specific master's degree.
- **Company examples**: HelloPM and JustAnotherPM give stories at Tesla, Amazon, Spotify, Netflix, Google and Salesforce (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]]). They read as illustrations, not documented case studies; don't cite them as facts about those companies.

## Questions to ask about your product

- Which type of PM does our biggest product risk call for right now?
- Is this role's customer a business, a consumer, or other teams?
- Do our PM titles mean the same thing across teams?
- Who owns positioning, the backlog and product operations, and is that written down?
