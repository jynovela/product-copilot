# User psychology and behavior

## What this covers

Persuasion, behavioral economics, choice design, desire and habits, as they apply to products and to people inside companies.

## Core ideas

General knowledge: a synthesis of the books below, from model memory.

- People decide with shortcuts and context more than with pure logic.
- How choices are presented (defaults, number of options, comparisons) changes what people pick.
- Adoption is behavior change: make the new behavior easy, attractive and social.
- These tools can manipulate; use them in the customer's interest.

## Concepts

- [[persuasion-principles]]: Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, liking, authority, scarcity and unity: why people say yes.
- [[choice-architecture]]: How options are presented (defaults, number, order, comparisons) steers decisions.
- [[mimetic-desire]]: People learn what to want by imitating others (models).
- [[rider-elephant-path]]: To change behavior, direct the rational rider, motivate the emotional elephant and shape the path.
- [[habit-design]]: Make good behaviors obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying.
- [[system-1-and-system-2]]: Fast, intuitive thinking versus slow, deliberate thinking, and the biases that come from the first.

## Questions to ask about your product

- What is the default, and who chose it?
- How many options do we show at the key decision point?
- What behavior must change for customers to adopt this?
