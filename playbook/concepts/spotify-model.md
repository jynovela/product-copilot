# Spotify model (squads, tribes, chapters, guilds)

## What it is

General knowledge: Described by Henrik Kniberg and Anders Ivarsson in 2012. Squads are small, autonomous, cross-functional teams owning a mission. Tribes group related squads. Chapters connect people with the same skill across squads. Guilds are communities of interest. It's an example of a team structure, not a framework to install.

## How to use it

- Borrow the principles, such as autonomy with alignment, rather than the names.
- Decide how squads align on strategy before granting autonomy.

## Watch out for

- Renaming teams to squads and calling it a transformation.
- Copying a structure that its own company kept changing.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Spotify's squads complete work in sprints, allowing them to test, iterate and release quickly. (see [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]])
- Autonomous, cross-functional squads choose what to work on toward an outcome, within the overall strategy. (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]])

## Contradictions

- Both sources present the model as Spotify's working practice (see [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]], [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: people who worked at Spotify have said the 2012 model was an aspiration that was never fully implemented as described, and that the company moved on from it. Confidence: medium.
