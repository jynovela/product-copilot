# Product Vision Board (Pichler)

## What it is

Roman Pichler's tool for capturing a product strategy on one page: the vision on top, and four strategy choices below it. Target group (who the users and customers are), needs (the main problem solved or job done), product (the 3 to 5 standout features that make people choose it) and business goals (revenue, cost, brand). His framework stacks vision (5 to 10 years, not measurable), strategy (12 to 24 months, reviewed at least every three months), roadmap (outcome-based, usually 12 months) and backlog, with links running both ways. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])

## How to use it

- Capture the strategy on the board and validate its biggest assumptions until none are major ("product strategy discovery"). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Derive one specific measurable outcome, decide what to build, and review goal and strategy after 2 to 3 months. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Put OKRs on the roadmap, not on the vision or strategy; use the needs and business goals to choose metrics. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Pichler recommends the PM with an extended team owns all four levels, with the final say if the team can't agree. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Strategy Stack for senior management: business strategy, portfolio strategy, technology and product strategy, roadmaps, backlog. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])

## Watch out for

- Standout features are not a mini backlog or wish list (Pichler). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Pichler sells training and books, so treat this as practitioner advice with a commercial interest. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])

## Contradictions

- Features in the strategy: Pichler lists 3 to 5 standout features; Hemnet and Perri say a strategy never names features. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
