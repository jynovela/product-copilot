# Jobs to be done (JTBD)

## What it is

General knowledge: Customers don't buy products; they hire them to get a job done in a particular circumstance. The job has functional, social and emotional sides. Competition is anything else that could be hired for the same job.

## How to use it

- Interview about the moment someone switched to or from a solution: what was happening, what they tried, what pushed them.
- Write the job as progress in a circumstance, e.g. "when I commute alone in the morning, help me stay full and entertained".
- Use jobs to define real competitors and to segment by situation.

## Watch out for

- Jobs written as features ("export to PDF") aren't jobs.
- Demographic segments hide the job.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Customers "hire" products for a job with functional, emotional and social sides; examples: Intercom, and Christensen's milkshake study. (see [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]])
- Focuses on scenarios rather than personas; championed by Clayton Christensen. (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]])
- Shifts the team's focus from the product to what customers want to get done. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- Reframes the problem around what customers need rather than what they want. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])
- Helps you understand why people use a product. (see [[linkedin-10-pm-frameworks-post]])
- The progress a customer is trying to make when hiring a product. (see [[2026-11-20-juan-pm-frameworks-list]])
- A job plus its circumstance; five steps (identify, define, understand circumstances, prioritize, design); break jobs into steps, needs and variables; template "When (situation), I want to (job), so I can (outcome)"; most teams miss the emotional and social jobs. (see [[2023-05-17-hellopm-jobs-to-be-done]])

## Contradictions

- This page says jobs written as features aren't jobs. HelloPM's example job statements name solutions, such as "I want to use Spotify..." and "I want to request a reliable ride" (see [[2023-05-17-hellopm-jobs-to-be-done]]). Keep the solution out of the job; the HelloPM examples read better as job stories about a specific product.
- HelloPM's "When..., I want to..., so I can..." template (see [[2023-05-17-hellopm-jobs-to-be-done]]) is the job story format. General knowledge: it came from Intercom and Alan Klement, not Christensen, and Ulwick's job statements use a different format (verb, object, context). The drill-and-hole example used by HelloPM and Product School (see [[2023-05-17-hellopm-jobs-to-be-done]], [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]]) is Theodore Levitt's, not Christensen's.
