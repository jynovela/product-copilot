# Competition-based pricing

## What it is

Set your price relative to competitors' prices for similar products: below, matching or above (premium). Also called competitive, competitor-based or market-based pricing, or price indexing. Dolansky (BDC) names three stances: co-operative (match), aggressive (stay below and cut when they cut) and dismissive (a leader that ignores rivals' prices). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Track competitors' prices constantly, including their price history (Unleashed, Qualtrics, Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Read the history as a signal: steady increases suggest inelastic demand and room for a premium entrant; frequent discounts signal a commoditized market (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Fits saturated mass-goods markets and mature, slow-growth markets where any move is matched (Unleashed, Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- If an audit finds no real differentiator: a leader may justify a small premium over the average, a non-leader a small discount, and a much weaker product should rework its proposition before pricing (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Price wars and margin erosion (several sources). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Customers see you as the same as rivals (LogRocket). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Product Management Journal says matching gives "a more stable market for everyone"; LogRocket says it makes you look the same as rivals. Both can hold. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
