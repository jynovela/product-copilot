# Eisenhower matrix

## What it is

General knowledge: A 2x2 of urgency against importance. Urgent and important: do now. Important but not urgent: schedule time for it, because this is where strategy, discovery and people work live. Urgent but not important: delegate. Neither: drop. The 4 Ds (do, defer, delegate, dump) are the same idea.

## How to use it

- Each week, block time for important but not urgent work first.
- Ask who else could do the urgent but unimportant items.

## Watch out for

- Living in the urgent quadrants.
- Calling everything important.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Two axes, urgent and important; work the important and urgent quadrant first. Also lists the 4 Ds: do, defer, delegate, dump. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- Important and not urgent is "tasks you want to do, but can wait". (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])
- For managing time and initiatives. (see [[linkedin-10-pm-frameworks-post]])
- Quadrants are do, schedule, delegate and delete; move more work into quadrant two (important, not urgent); watch for the mere-urgency effect, where deadlines make tasks feel more important than they are. (see [[asana-eisenhower-matrix-guide]])

## Contradictions

- Techreviewer describes important but not urgent work as tasks that "can wait" (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]). Asana's guide says to schedule it and move more work into that quadrant (see [[asana-eisenhower-matrix-guide]]), which matches Covey's version (general knowledge).
