# Technical product manager (TPM)

## What it is

General knowledge: A product manager whose product can't be understood without technical depth: APIs, infrastructure, developer tools, data platforms, payments or security. The job is still product management (problem, customer, value, prioritization), with more time spent on technical requirements, architecture trade-offs and technical debt. Many TPMs were engineers, but the title says what the product needs, not what degree the person has. TPM is also the usual short form for technical program manager, a delivery-coordination role that is not product management.

## How to use it

- Use the title when the customer or the key decisions are technical, such as developers using an API.
- Hire for the product's technical depth, not for coding ability; the TPM's output is decisions and requirements, not code.
- Pair a TPM with someone strong on market and customer if the product also sells to non-technical buyers.

## Watch out for

- A TPM who turns into an engineering manager or architect and stops talking to customers.
- Confusing technical product manager with technical program manager in job ads and org charts.
- Asking non-technical PMs to own deeply technical platforms without support.

- Has a developer or engineering background; duties include defining technical requirements, prioritizing by feasibility and conducting code reviews. (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]])

## Contradictions

- Do TPMs code? Agilemania lists conducting code reviews as a TPM duty (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]]). ProdPad says TPMs usually don't code (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]), and JustAnotherPM says PMs don't need to code (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]]). General knowledge: code review is engineering work; a TPM who does it regularly is doing a different job.
- Is a technical background required? JustAnotherPM says it is a must and the coaching platform says TPMs almost always come from engineering (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]], [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]]). Ally Mexicotte says a TPM can be a PM who simply loves the technical side, and the online school says the title describes the person filling a PM role (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]], [[online-school-types-of-product-manager]]).
- How strategic is a TPM? The coaching platform says TPMs focus on capabilities, see customers less and are often product owners, and Mitchell (via Stanford Online) says they may lack strategic insight (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]], [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]]). Recruiting from Scratch says TPMs own the vision and roadmap and must understand the market, and Product School says most of their duties match any PM (see [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]]).
