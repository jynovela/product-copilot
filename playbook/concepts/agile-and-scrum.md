# Agile and Scrum

## What it is

General knowledge: Agile is a set of values from the 2001 Agile Manifesto: working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. Scrum is the most common Agile method: fixed sprints of one to four weeks, a product backlog, sprint planning, daily stand-ups, a review and a retrospective. Kanban limits work in progress instead of using sprints. Other methods include XP, DSDM, FDD, Crystal, RAD and the scaling frameworks such as SAFe. Lean Software Development applies lean manufacturing principles to software. Waterfall runs phases in sequence and suits work with fixed, well-known requirements.

## How to use it

- Use sprints to ship and learn, not just to keep busy.
- Keep the backlog short and ordered.
- Pair delivery with discovery so the backlog holds tested ideas.

## Watch out for

- Agile rituals with no customer feedback ("agile theater").
- A backlog that becomes a junkyard of requests.
- Scaling frameworks that add process faster than value.

## Contradictions

- Techreviewer offers Scrum or Kanban as alternatives to Agile (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: both are Agile methods.
- ProductPlan says Lean Software Development was originally called the Toyota Production System (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]]), and Techreviewer says the Software Engineering Institute developed it in 1997 (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: the Toyota Production System is the manufacturing origin of lean thinking. Lean Software Development is Mary and Tom Poppendieck's 2003 book that applied it to software.
