# Growth product manager

## What it is

General knowledge: A growth PM is measured on a stage of the funnel (acquisition, activation, retention, referral, monetization) rather than on a feature area. The work is finding the biggest drop-off, forming hypotheses, and running experiments, often A/B tests, with engineering, design, data and marketing. Growth work pays off once a product has product-market fit and enough traffic to test; before that, the job is finding fit.

## How to use it

- Give the growth PM one metric and the authority to change any surface that moves it.
- Check traffic before promising A/B tests; low volume means slow or inconclusive tests.
- Agree with core product teams who owns which screens, so growth experiments don't break the core experience.

## Watch out for

- Short-term wins that hurt long-term retention or trust, such as dark patterns.
- Hiring a growth PM before product-market fit.
- Treating growth as marketing with a product title.

- Owns a metric rather than a whole product and runs a series of experiments, working at a micro rather than macro level. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- Delivers business outcomes across growth, retention and revenue, working with business leaders. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])

- Expands the user base and adoption through data, A/B tests and onboarding, working closely with marketing, sales and customer success. (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]])
- Leads experiments on new features in existing products; startups and businesses seeking profit hire them. (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]])
- Drives acquisition, engagement and retention through referral programs, viral loops and content (example: Clash of Clans referrals). (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]])
- Laser-focused on acquisition, activation, retention, referrals and revenue with their product, through A/B tests and continuous improvement. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- Scales products through acquisition, retention and monetization; common at SaaS companies with freemium models. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Works on a product that already has product-market fit, owns one growth metric, and needs enough weekly users for statistically sound A/B tests. (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])
- Solves growth issues such as demand or supply with data, under clear KPIs and high expectations. (see [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]])
- Focuses on acquiring and retaining users with data (example: comparing influencer and banner-ad users by lifetime value at Spotify). (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]])
- Doesn't own a specific product; works on the growth of one or more products alongside the PMs who own them. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- Grows the user base and metrics through experiments, often on a team that includes sales, marketing and UX; the product is the funnel. (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- Spends much of the time on market analysis with product marketing, competitive intelligence and partners to find new opportunities and markets. (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]])
- Works on KPIs and short-term targets to raise revenue; needs sales, marketing and research skills. (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- The growth archetype has marketing and channel skills, loves experiments, and favors speed over craft; less good at zero-to-one work. (see [[2024-03-05-adam-fishman-pm-archetypes]])

## Contradictions

- Recruiting from Scratch's summary says growth PMs focus on promoting and selling the product, but its own detailed section and most other sources describe metric ownership through experiments (see [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]). Selling is sales and marketing work.
- Does a growth PM own a product? Stanford Online says growth PMs don't own a specific product and Product School says they own a metric instead (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]]). ProdPad says they drive metrics with their product (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]).
- LogRocket describes growth PMs mainly as finding new markets through market analysis with product marketing and competitive intelligence (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]]). Most other sources describe funnel experiments inside the existing product (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]], [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]]). General knowledge: new-market work is closer to what Winters and Mosavat call product-market fit expansion (see [[types-of-product-work]]).
- Ally Mexicotte says growth PM work needs product-market fit and enough user volume for A/B tests (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]]). Product Faculty says startups hire growth PMs, and Recruiting from Scratch calls them essential for startups seeking rapid expansion (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]]). Inference: both can hold if the startup already has fit and traffic.
