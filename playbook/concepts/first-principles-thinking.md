# First principles thinking

## What it is

General knowledge: Instead of copying what others do, list the basic facts and constraints of a problem and build a solution from them. It helps when the usual answer comes from habit rather than from the problem.

## How to use it

- Ask which parts of the current approach are true constraints and which are habit.
- Rebuild the cost or the flow from its basic parts.

## Watch out for

- Using it to ignore what others have already learned.
- Taking far longer than reasoning by analogy needs.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Listed as a way to become a clear thinker and problem solver. (see [[linkedin-10-pm-frameworks-post]])
- Three habits: break systems into real parts, recombine parts across systems, question analogies. Expensive, so save it for high-stakes or stuck problems; use analogy and testing for small, reversible calls. (see [[2026-04-22-hellopm-first-principles]])
- Case studies: SpaceX rocket costs, Cursor rebuilding the editor around AI, DeepSeek's training costs, Amazon's services becoming AWS. (see [[2026-04-22-hellopm-first-principles]])

## Contradictions

- HelloPM presents DeepSeek-V3's reported $5.58 million as the cost of training the model (see [[2026-04-22-hellopm-first-principles]]). General knowledge: DeepSeek's own report said that figure covered only the final training run and excluded earlier research and experiments, so it isn't the full cost. Confidence: medium.
