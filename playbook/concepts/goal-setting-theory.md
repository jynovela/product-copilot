# Goal-setting theory

## What it is

Edwin Locke and Gary Latham's finding that specific, challenging goals beat "do your best" goals, time-bound goals beat open-ended ones, and individual goals aligned with group goals help team performance. Growthspace cites 35 years and 400+ studies. The digest calls this the one solid evidence base in the batch; the percentages vendors quote are not checked. Locke's five factors of an effective goal: clarity, challenge, commitment, feedback, task complexity. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])

## How to use it

- Make goals specific and hard but believable. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Give feedback during the cycle; visible small progress drives motivation (Amabile and Kramer's progress principle, via Growthspace). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Implementation intentions ("if X happens, I will do Y") are said to make follow-through 2 to 3 times more likely (James Clear, citing a study). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Plan obstacles: write the success story, then list obstacles and responses (Donadio). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])

## Watch out for

- Stretch goals can push unethical behaviour (Blanchard) and "can be crushing if people don't believe they're achievable" (Doerr). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- The 11 to 25% performance gain quoted by vendors isn't checked. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- The often-quoted "Dominican University" written-goals study is cited as 42%, 30% and 40% by different sources; it is a small study, often misquoted. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
