# TAM, SAM and SOM

## What it is

TAM (total addressable market) is revenue at 100% share, the theoretical ceiling. SAM (serviceable addressable or available market) is the part your model, pricing, geography and capabilities can serve. SOM (serviceable obtainable market) is what you can realistically win in one to three years; noirbook calls it "the number that should drive resource allocation". (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## How to use it

- Build bottom-up: companies that fit × share with the problem × price × realistic capture. noirbook's illustration: 50,000 × 30% × $12K × 5% = $9M. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Productboard's prompt: count the ideal customers, model beachhead revenue, expand to SAM by layers as a range, then TAM with caveats. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Run three sanity tests: does your plan imply your claimed market? competitor revenue ÷ their share; buyers' annual category spend × number of buyers (Productboard). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Look beyond size: growth rate, timing, penetration, buyer psychology, competitive intensity (Productboard). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Watch out for

- Never present a percentage of a huge market as your opportunity (Productboard). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- This wiki keeps market size off problem pages; it belongs on strategy or analysis pages. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Contradictions

- Top-down sizing: Productboard calls it useless; Olafioye says it is fine for framing if checked bottom-up; noirbook says TAM is for investors, not prioritization. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
