# Cost-plus pricing

## What it is

Add a markup to the unit cost: price = unit cost × (1 + markup %). A margin-based variant is price = variable cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Also called markup or cost-based pricing; named by nearly every source, and called the most common strategy overall by Melio and by HubSpot data cited by Nationwide. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Count the full cost first, including labour and overheads that are easy to miss (Unleashed). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Keep markup and margin apart: markup is profit ÷ cost, margin is profit ÷ price. A 35% markup is a 25.9% margin. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Fits physical goods, commodities, retail and small firms without a research budget (Unleashed, Gupta, Melio). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Open-book cost-plus (costs shown, a fixed margin agreed) appears in professional services and government contracts (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Ignores demand and competitors; if your cost falls, your price and revenue fall with it (Melio). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Removes the incentive to control cost and pushes sales to discount (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Gupta calls it "fundamentally broken for software", because marginal cost is near zero. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Shopify calls a 35% markup a "35% profit margin" and Aha! calls $10 to $15 a "50 percent margin"; both mix up markup and margin. The real margins are 25.9% and 33%. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
