# Problem awareness ladder

## What it is

Steve Blank's categories, as told by Ujjwal Trivedi: latent (they have the problem but don't know it), passive (they know but aren't trying to fix it), active (looking for a fix, not burning) and urgent ("they'll buy if you're there"); Trivedi adds imaginary problems, fixed by communication rather than product. General knowledge, unverified: Blank's own ladder in *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* has five observable steps: has a problem, knows it, is actively looking, has cobbled together a workaround, has or can get budget. Trivedi also contrasts painkillers, vitamins and candy. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## How to use it

- Place a problem on the ladder from what people do, not what they say. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Problems move between steps over time; a shock can make a latent problem urgent overnight (Trivedi). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Match positioning to the type: a painkiller sold as a vitamin won't sell (Trivedi). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- This wiki's Urgency line on problem pages uses the five-step version because each step can be observed. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Watch out for

- Urgent problems usually already have competitors; latent ones need years of education before they sell (Trivedi). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Contradictions

- ProdPad says more severe pain means a more valuable solution; Trivedi says how people act on the problem matters more than how much it hurts. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
