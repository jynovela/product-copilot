# Value-based pricing

## What it is

Price on the value the customer gets or perceives, not on your cost. Named by every source. Gupta calls it "the gold standard for most modern B2B SaaS"; Paddle, Product School, ProdPad and Qualtrics agree for software and B2B. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Dolansky's steps (BDC): pick the product the customer would compare yours with and its price; list how yours differs; put a money value on each difference, adding positives and subtracting negatives; check the result is above your cost; explain the price. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Economic value to the customer (PMA): next best alternative $50 + insole $10 + $15 for the built-in feature = $75; the price can differ by segment. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- ROI framing (Gupta): a feature that saves 10 hours per rep per month at a loaded $75 an hour is worth $750, so $50 per rep per month is an easy yes; put an ROI calculator in sales material. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Objective price, then perception (Product Management Journal): start from the money value of the benefit; opportunity value and brand push the price up, migration cost, uncertainty and inertia push it down, which is why trials and promotions exist. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- It needs research, is partly guesswork, and is harder to keep stable (Melio, Unleashed, PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Customers don't value every feature, and brand value is hard to price (Investopedia). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
