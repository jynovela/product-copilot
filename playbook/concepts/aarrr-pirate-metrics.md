# AARRR (pirate metrics)

## What it is

General knowledge: Dave McClure's 2007 model splits the customer lifecycle into five stages with a metric for each: acquisition (users arrive), activation (first good experience), retention (they come back), referral (they bring others) and revenue (they pay). Find the leakiest stage and work there. Many argue retention should be fixed first.

## How to use it

- Define activation as a specific action that predicts retention.
- Measure stage-to-stage conversion by cohort.
- Find the weakest stage, but watch every stage when you change one; they affect each other.

## Watch out for

- Pouring money into acquisition while retention leaks.
- Vague definitions of activation.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Designed for startups by Dave McClure: acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- Breaks the user journey into stages. (see [[linkedin-10-pm-frameworks-post]])
- Tracks the user lifecycle across five stages. (see [[2026-11-20-juan-pm-frameworks-list]])
- From McClure's 2007 Ignite Seattle talk; activation is the "aha" moment (Dropbox: one file uploaded); McClure suggested simple activation measures such as page views or 30 seconds on site. (see [[2020-05-07-built-in-aarrr-metrics]])
- In SaaS, retention and revenue often swap places. Reforge's Andrew Chen and Brian Balfour call AARRR too micro and say it siloes the stages; a growth lead quoted says the stages are interdependent and shouldn't be tested one at a time. Retention is now seen as the key metric. (see [[2020-05-07-built-in-aarrr-metrics]])

## What the research digests say

- Acquisition: CAC = sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers, segmented by channel (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- Activation: find the early actions that correlate with long-term retention, such as Dropbox's one file in one folder on one device (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- Retention: cohort curves; a curve that flattens signals product-market fit (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- Referral: K-factor = invites per user × invite conversion; above 1.0 is viral and rare (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- Revenue: LTV and LTV:CAC; "LTV at least 3× CAC" is an unsourced rule of thumb. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- Variants reorder or rename the stages (acquisition, activation, conversion, engagement, revenue, retention). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])

## Contradictions

- Classic advice is to find the leakiest stage and work on it alone. Built In quotes a growth lead and Reforge saying the stages are interdependent and shouldn't be treated separately (see [[2020-05-07-built-in-aarrr-metrics]]). Both can hold: find the weakest stage, but measure every stage when you change one.
