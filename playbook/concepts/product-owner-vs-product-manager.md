# Product owner vs product manager

## What it is

General knowledge: Product owner is an accountability in Scrum: one person who orders the product backlog and maximizes the value of the team's work. Product manager is a job title that covers the whole product: customers, market, strategy, discovery and business results. Some companies give both to one person; others split them, with the PM facing outward and the PO working inside the team. Marty Cagan argues the PO responsibilities should belong to the PM.

## How to use it

- If you split the roles, write down who decides what gets built and who talks to customers.
- If one person does both, protect time for discovery; backlog work fills every gap.

## Watch out for

- A PO who only writes tickets for decisions made elsewhere.
- Hiring a PO and expecting product strategy without giving the authority.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Product owner is listed among PM roles: supports the development team by prioritizing the backlog and writing user stories. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- Similar to PMs but in agile settings with a narrower, internal scope: backlog, user stories and sprint value, while PMs look to market data. (see [[pragmatic-institute-pm-job-titles]])
- Product owner is a product-adjacent role outside the PM hierarchy that manages the backlog with engineering. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- Product owner is an adjacent role, not a type of PM. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- Technical PMs are often the product owners on their teams. (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])

## Contradictions

- Aha! lists product owner as a PM role, while Product School and ProdPad call it adjacent and not a PM (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]). General knowledge: in Scrum, product owner is an accountability, not a job title, so both views exist in practice.
