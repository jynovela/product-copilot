# Pretotyping

## What it is

Testing whether people want something before building it, with cheap market-engagement tests. Paweł Huryn's library (building on Alberto Savoia's pretotyping techniques) lists: email, paid-ad and social campaigns; landing page; fake door; facade; Pinocchio; explainer video; pre-order; letter of intent; provincial (small first market); pop-up; infiltrator (in someone else's channel); crowdfunding. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])

## How to use it

- Start here, then move to minimal products, then prototypes, then production experiments (Huryn's ladder). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- Prefer tests where people give something real, such as money, a signature or time; that "skin in the game" is stronger evidence (Huryn). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- Use the XYZ hypothesis: "At least X% of Y will do Z" (Huryn). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- Letters of intent are strong evidence for B2B (Huryn). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])

## Watch out for

- Crowdfunding backers may not be your customers (Huryn). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- A smaller version of the product isn't a good test artefact; design the artefact to test the hypothesis (Strategyzer). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
