# AI product manager

## What it is

General knowledge: An AI PM decides where AI solves a real customer problem and then manages what makes AI products different: outputs that vary and can be wrong, dependence on training and evaluation data, cost and latency trade-offs, and ethics, bias and privacy. The craft includes defining evaluation sets and quality bars, designing for errors, and knowing when a simpler non-AI solution is better. Many sources expect AI skills to become part of every PM's job.

## How to use it

- Start from the customer problem and test whether AI beats a simpler approach.
- Define how you'll evaluate output quality before building, and keep a test set.
- Design the experience for wrong answers: confidence cues, undo, human review.

## Watch out for

- Adding AI as a pitch-deck feature with no customer problem behind it.
- Shipping without evaluations, then learning about failures from users.
- Ignoring cost per request until usage grows.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Grouped with data PMs: analytics, pipelines, ML models and AI features. (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]])
- Identifies AI/ML opportunities, defines requirements, works with data scientists and ensures ethical use; more PMs will need AI skills. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- Builds products with data scientists that automate, personalize and derive insights (example: Google Health readmission prediction). (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]])
- Makes sure AI solves real problems rather than adding a buzzword; manages variability, prompt engineering, hallucinations, bias and data security; a fast-rising type. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- Integrates AI and ML into products; needs a solid base in AI/ML concepts; listed as an emerging specialization. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Manages AI products from chatbots to self-driving cars, with ethics and customer focus; must be comfortable with data and ML (example: Netflix recommendations). (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]])
- A good start for data scientists, but AI is a tool; the goal is still the customer's problem. A data scientist she knew went back to data science because PMs spend little time on AI itself. (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- Works with data science on models and predictions and needs technical and data skills (example: Google Assistant). (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]])
- A highly technical subspecialty like AI or ML may need an engineering background. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])

## Contradictions

- Creately lists both a data PM who leads ML and AI-driven decisions and a separate AI/ML PM, without saying where one ends (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]]). The user's summary merges data and AI into one type, while ProdPad keeps them separate (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]).
