# Kill criteria

## What it is

Annie Duke's kill criteria, set during a premortem: signals that things aren't going as planned, each tied to an action decided in advance. Example: if there's no first sales deal within 3 months of launch, kill the product (Abraham, citing Duke and First Round Review). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])

## How to use it

- Write them when you commit, while you can still think clearly about failure. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])
- Before reopening a decision, ask what new information is now known; that stops flip-flopping but allows a change of course (Rekhi). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])
- Duke's three Ps for the decision itself: preferences, payoffs and probabilities, laid out in a decision tree. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])

## Watch out for

- Duke says good decisions need a willingness to guess probabilities; the 6-frameworks piece says inaccurate probabilities are the main pitfall of decision trees. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])

## Contradictions

- Reopening: Bain sets a high hurdle; PM-2025 and Chakraborty say few decisions are permanent; Rekhi reopens when you can name new information. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-decisions]])
