# Product operations

## What it is

General knowledge: General knowledge: product operations (popularized by Melissa Perri and Denise Tilles) supports product teams so PMs spend more time on product decisions. Typical areas are business and product data, customer and market insight, tools and processes (planning, launches, reviews), and governance. It usually appears once a company has many product teams. It is not running the product day to day, and it is not a PM role.

## How to use it

- Start product ops with one painful problem, such as scattered data or chaotic launches.
- Measure it by PM time saved and decision quality, not process count.

## Watch out for

- Product ops that becomes process police.
- Using product ops to make up for PMs who can't get customer access.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Empowers the core product team by streamlining processes, improving collaboration and documenting best practices. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- A product-adjacent role that streamlines processes, data and tools so product teams can focus on building. (see [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]])
- Supports PMs with structure for interviews, QA, experiments, tools and data; probably only at large companies; she has not met anyone in the role. (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])
- Optimizes product processes and workflows and acts as a communication hub. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Focuses on managing the day-to-day operations of the product. (see [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]])
- Manages day-to-day operations and product data to make operations more efficient; placed in a business group focused on marketing. (see [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]])
- Manages day-to-day operations of the product and business and uses product data to improve them. (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
- An adjacent role, not a type of PM. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])

## What Escaping the Build Trap adds

Source note: from secondary summaries of the book, not the book text. Chapter numbers are the book's.

- Perri's version starts as a very small team, two people in her example,
  that runs the strategy cadence, sets up analytics tracking and compiles
  progress toward goals into reports for executives (Build Trap ch20). In
  large companies it reports to the CPO under a VP-level leader (ch20).
- Its duties (ch20):
  - Automate collecting data on progress toward goals across teams.
  - Report goals, outcomes, roadmaps, progress, capacity and cost, and
    translate them into financial terms for executives.
  - Run the product analytics platform.
  - Standardize cross-team processes: strategy cadences, experiment
    tracking, feature documentation, goal setting, roadmaps and sales
    enablement.
  - Run the key meetings for strategy, strategy deployment and releases.
    See [[strategy-deployment]].
  - Coach and train product teams.
- It sets the inputs and outputs of the work, not how teams work. It builds
  the roadmap template, not the roadmap, and helps teams find users to test
  with rather than deciding whether they may talk to users (ch20).
- Staff it with project managers and product people, plus a few developers
  for integrations and custom tools (ch20).

## Contradictions

- Aha!, Product School, Ally Mexicotte and Creately describe product ops as support for product teams (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]], [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]]). Recruiting from Scratch, Product Faculty and the coaching platform describe it as running the day-to-day operations of the product or business (see [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]], [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]]). General knowledge: the first view matches how the term is used in product-ops writing.
- Creately and Product Faculty list product ops as a type of PM, while ProdPad and Product School say it is adjacent (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[product-faculty-types-of-product-managers]], [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]], [[2024-04-03-product-school-18-types-of-pms]]).
- Perri's account backs the support view: product ops builds systems and
  reports for product teams and does not run the product (Build Trap
  ch20).
