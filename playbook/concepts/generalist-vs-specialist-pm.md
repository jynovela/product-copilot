# Generalist vs specialist PM

## What it is

General knowledge: A generalist PM owns a product end to end: discovery, strategy, delivery, launch and the business. A specialist PM goes deep on one dimension, such as growth, technical depth, data or a domain. Specialist titles mostly say which part of the job matters most in that role; every PM still does all of it to some degree. Startups usually need generalists; larger companies with many teams can afford specialists.

## How to use it

- Early in a career, a generalist role shows you which parts of the job you enjoy and are good at.
- Specialize when you are clearly strong in one area and the market pays for it, and keep the core skills fresh.
- When hiring, write the job ad around the main risk of the product, not a fashionable title.

## Watch out for

- Specialist titles that hide a generalist job, or the reverse.
- Specializing so early that you can't run a product end to end.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Every PM does what the specialist types do; a type just singles out the focus that matters most to the hiring company. To be good, be all types at once: diversify, don't specialize. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- Startups need versatile PMs; mature companies benefit from specialists such as technical or growth PMs. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Specialized PMs bring deeper expertise and make teams more efficient, as doctors specialize. (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]])
- Demand for specialized PMs will only grow. (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]])
- Start as a generalist to learn every aspect, then consider specializing once you have launched products. (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]])
- A generalist knows the end-to-end lifecycle and can lead teams in any industry or domain; domain or industry expertise can be a superpower in a tough job market. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- The generalist wears many hats across the lifecycle (example: launching Airbnb Experiences). (see [[2024-04-15-hellopm-types-of-product-managers]])
- Types are not mutually exclusive; one person can fit several. (see [[2022-08-31-tiwatayo-kunle-types-of-pms]])
- Lumping all PM roles together hides which skills and frameworks work in which context. (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])

## Contradictions

- ProdPad argues PMs should diversify rather than specialize (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]]). JustAnotherPM and Agilemania argue specialization makes teams more efficient and will grow in demand (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]], [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]]). LogRocket and Creately take a middle line: generalist first or at startups, specialist later or at larger companies (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]]).
