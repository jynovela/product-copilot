# Price skimming

## What it is

Launch at a high price for early adopters, then lower it in steps to reach more price-sensitive buyers. The name comes from skimming cream (Qualtrics). Examples given: iPhone, game consoles, pharma, Salesforce's early CRM. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Fits innovative, anticipated products with no real rival or a strong moat (Unleashed, Gupta), or a highly differentiated product with limited supply (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Plan the step-down schedule; Gupta suggests lowering over 6 to 18 months. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- When rivals arrive, defend with lower-end versions (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Track ARPU, gross margin and conversion at each price (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Copycats; early buyers feel cheated if the price drops soon after launch; buyers learn to wait (Unleashed, Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Works mainly for brands seen as innovative or exclusive. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Indeed's employer content describes skimming as market-driven produce prices, which is wrong; don't use that definition. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
