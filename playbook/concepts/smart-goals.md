# SMART goals

## What it is

The most cited goal checklist: Specific, Measurable, Achievable (or Attainable), Relevant, Time-bound. Doran's 1981 original was Specific, Measurable, Assignable, Realistic, Time-related. Variants swap in Actionable or Rewarding; SMARTIE adds Inclusive and Equitable; PACT, CRAFT, HARD and FUN are other acronyms. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])

## How to use it

- Use it as a filter on a goal already drafted, not to generate one (Growthspace, Fechter). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Fechter's PM reading: Measurable means indicator, baseline, target and data source agreed before work starts; Achievable means within the team's authority; Time-bound means a review or decision date, not only a ship date. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Also used as a final check on problem statements and KPIs (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]]).

## Watch out for

- It says nothing about whether the goal is the right one, and it can cap ambition (FranklinCovey, Productboard). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- Many "SMART" examples in the research name a solution, such as "Launch new dashboard to 100% of users by end of Q3" (Meegle). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
- It says nothing about whether the metric is causal or gameable (Manheim, see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]]). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]])
