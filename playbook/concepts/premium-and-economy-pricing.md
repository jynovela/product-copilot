# Premium and economy pricing

## What it is

Premium (prestige) pricing deliberately charges more than rivals and trades on quality and brand; economy pricing keeps cost and price low by design and relies on volume. Kotler's price-quality matrix (ProdPad, Aha!) names four positions: high price and high quality is premium, low and low is economy, high price and low quality is rip-off, low price and high quality is superb value. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Premium needs brand and quality that are built and kept up; margins are higher (Qualtrics). Examples given: McKinsey, Starbucks vs Dunkin', Tesla. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Economy works at scale and in downturns (Unleashed). Examples given: Walmart, Dollar Shave Club. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Inference: penetration is low for now; economy is low by design.

## Watch out for

- Bargain hunters switch easily and economy revenue needs volume (Unleashed). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- A price that is too low can signal low quality (Qualtrics, Unleashed). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Pricing in a category tends toward the average as markets mature and decline (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
