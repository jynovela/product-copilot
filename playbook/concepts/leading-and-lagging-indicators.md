# Leading and lagging indicators

## What it is

Leading indicators predict and can be moved now; lagging indicators confirm results later. Almost every source uses the split. Examples: pipeline volume vs quarterly revenue (Asana); burning 750 extra calories a day vs losing 15 pounds (LSA); trial signups vs MRR (The Product Space). Businesses are valued on lagging headline metrics, so teams need a leading metric that is responsive but still a direct driver of the lagging one (Hustle Badger). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])

## How to use it

- Decide upfront which one decides success for a test or goal (GrowthBook; see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]]).
- Use proxies when the real metric is slow: day-1 or day-7 retention for 30-day retention, ARPU for LTV (Digit, ProductPeople; see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- Ask which customer behaviours you must encourage to reach the business goal (Herbig; see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-goals]]).
- The same metric can lag for one function and lead for another: recruiting metrics lag for HR and lead for the business (NetSuite). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])

## Watch out for

- Most metrics are lagging, and lagging ones are the easiest to collect (NetSuite). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
- A leading metric only helps if it really drives the lagging one; check the link and refresh it (12 Characteristics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-metrics]])
