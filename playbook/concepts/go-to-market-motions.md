# Go-to-market motions

## What it is

A go-to-market motion is how the product is sold and reaches customers. Product School lists sales-led (outbound, demos, multi-stakeholder deals), product-led (self-serve signup, in-product upgrades, usage-driven expansion), hybrid (self-serve for small customers, sales-assisted for large) and partner- or ecosystem-led (marketplaces, integrations, resellers). PMA adds growth models: the funnel (marketing owns the top, sales the bottom), HubSpot's 2018 flywheel (customers at the center, referrals) and account-based marketing (a few high-value accounts treated as markets of one). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])

## How to use it

- Match the motion to the product, price point and buying process; a mismatched motion is a main cause of GTM failure (Dovetail). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- A dedicated sales team pays off only when the average purchase value is high, usually B2B (Kaizen). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- If the model depends on a free trial, onboarding, in-product education and upgrade prompts are central to GTM (Product School). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- "Most GTM strategies end up as a hybrid": product-led for self-serve customers plus account-based for accounts big enough to justify sales (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])

## Watch out for

- Taxonomies mix things up: Airtable's seven motions mix motions with channels (inbound, outbound, events); PMA mixes growth models, motions and tactics. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
