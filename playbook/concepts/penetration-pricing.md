# Penetration pricing

## What it is

Enter the market at a low price to win share quickly, then raise prices later. Named by most sources. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Fits crowded markets, network-effect and product-led products (Gupta), and high demand that rivals could copy easily (LogRocket). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Decide upfront how long you will run it and whether the early loss is sustainable (Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Track the metrics that match it: daily active users, market share and CAC payback (Gupta). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Profit usually comes only with scale, so it needs an "iron grip" on cost (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Price buyers are "the most fickle" and leave for the next cheaper offer; 1980s Japanese electronics firms were undercut and had to move to quality and brand (Product Management Journal). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Customers come to expect low prices, prices are hard to raise later, and some startups don't survive the early losses (Melio, Unleashed, Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Salesforce says penetration suits SaaS and Gupta ties it to product-led growth; Paddle steers SaaS away from economy and dynamic pricing. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
