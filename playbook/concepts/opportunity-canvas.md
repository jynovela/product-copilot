# Opportunity canvas

## What it is

noirbook's canvas for one opportunity: problem (evidence-based), users affected (who and how many), current alternatives, strategic fit, estimated impact (quantified), estimated effort (T-shirt size), confidence level per line, and "what would change your mind?". (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## How to use it

- Size by reach, frequency, depth, cost to the user and cost to the business, plus existing workarounds (Schugart, Trivedi, ProdPad, Stewart). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Get the numbers from instrumented data, support tickets, sales calls and the people closest to the problem; aim for "the right problem, not just the most vocal one" (Miro). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Use proxies when data is missing: a similar past feature's adoption, another team's click-through on the same surface (Belitsky). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Belitsky's idea sizing writes each idea as "By [project], [what will happen], leading to [effect on the North Star]", and keeps a shared book of all sized ideas. Sizing is "not a financial report, a thesis or a promise". (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Frame formally for work over two weeks, a new direction or a disagreement; skip it for known bugs and small validated work (noirbook). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Watch out for

- This wiki keeps evidence strength apart from magnitude and puts no RICE score on problem pages. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])

## Contradictions

- Score or compare: Torres (2016) says spreadsheet scoring treats an ill-structured problem as if a formula could solve it; Belitsky says a sizing spreadsheet against one North Star is the best way to rank ideas. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
