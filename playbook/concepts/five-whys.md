# Five whys

## What it is

General knowledge: A root-cause technique from Toyota. Starting from a problem, ask why it happened, then why that cause happened, and keep going until you reach something you can fix at the system level. Five is a guide, not a rule.

## How to use it

- Use it in blameless retrospectives after incidents or failed launches.
- Fix something at each level, sized to the problem.
- Write the chain down so others can check the logic.

## Watch out for

- Stopping at "someone made a mistake".
- Following one chain when there were several causes.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Listed as a way to get to the root of a problem. (see [[linkedin-10-pm-frameworks-post]])
- Five is a guide; three may be enough or you may need more. Use it for root causes, user motivations, sales requests and competitor research; listen more, ask open-ended why questions. (see [[2023-05-16-hellopm-5-whys]])
- Use it before choosing a solution: in their example, low activation traced to a paywall placed too early, not to missing onboarding videos. (see [[2026-04-22-hellopm-first-principles]])

## What the research digests say

- Ask why about each answer, usually about five times, until you reach a cause you can't explain further (Barrows, Morelli, Miro, noirbook). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- It can push you to one cause when several contribute; use it to form hypotheses and check them with data (noirbook). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-problem-size]])
- Ben Smith uses it to find the one underlying assumption a hypothesis depends on (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]]).
