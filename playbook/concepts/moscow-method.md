# MoSCoW method

## What it is

General knowledge: From DSDM (Dai Clegg). Must-haves are what the release can't go without. Should-haves are important but not vital for this timebox. Could-haves are nice to have and dropped first. Won't-haves are agreed out of scope this time. It agrees scope for a fixed timebox; it doesn't rank items.

## How to use it

- Use it with non-technical stakeholders to agree a release scope.
- Cap must-haves, for example at about 60% of effort, so there's room to drop work.

## Watch out for

- Everything becomes a must-have.
- Using it to rank items inside a bucket; it can't.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- A four-step process for prioritizing requirements around return on investment; frames discussions around revenue; the meaning of "won't have" is unclear. (see [[atlassian-6-prioritization-frameworks]])
- Should-haves are important but not time-sensitive and belong in the next release; teams overinflate must-haves; better for release criteria than ranking. (see [[tempo-9-prioritization-frameworks]])
- Helps stakeholders understand importance within a specific release. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- Aligns stakeholders on scope for a release or timeframe. (see [[2026-11-20-juan-pm-frameworks-list]])
- Centers team and stakeholder input; doesn't explicitly consider revenue or workload; set tie-breaking rules first. (see [[2025-07-04-contentsquare-prioritization-frameworks]])

## Contradictions

- Atlassian frames MoSCoW as prioritizing around return on investment and revenue (see [[atlassian-6-prioritization-frameworks]]). Contentsquare says it doesn't explicitly consider revenue (see [[2025-07-04-contentsquare-prioritization-frameworks]]), and the other sources, like DSDM where it comes from (general knowledge), use it to agree scope for one release or timebox.
- Tempo says should-haves "belong in the next release" (see [[tempo-9-prioritization-frameworks]]). General knowledge: in DSDM, should-haves are still aimed at the current timebox; they're just not vital to it. Atlassian calls "won't have" unclear (see [[atlassian-6-prioritization-frameworks]]); in DSDM it means "won't have this time".
