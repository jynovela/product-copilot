# Platform product manager

## What it is

General knowledge: A platform PM builds the things other products are built on: shared services (identity, payments, search, data), internal tools, developer APIs, or a partner ecosystem. The customers are usually internal teams or outside developers, so success looks like their speed, reliability and adoption rather than end-user features. The hard parts are prioritizing between many internal customers, saying no, and making build-vs-buy calls.

## How to use it

- Name your platform's customers and measure their outcomes (time to integrate, incidents, adoption).
- Run discovery with internal teams the way you would with external customers.
- Publish a roadmap and a request process so other teams can plan around you.

## Watch out for

- Becoming a ticket queue for every team's requests.
- Building a platform before two or more products need the same thing.
- Calling every infrastructure project a platform.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Builds shared APIs, internal tools or infrastructure that enable other teams or external developers. (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]])
- Creates and optimizes technical components shared across multiple products, working with engineering. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- Builds platforms that support multiple products, often APIs and core infrastructure; must be scalable, secure and easy to integrate. (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]])
- Oversees internal platforms, APIs and infrastructure, including integration with external partners. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Scales multiple products on a platform, is further from the end user, handles build vs buy, and works at larger companies; titles include trust and security, identity, data and ML PM. (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])
- Creates technical components for multiple products and cross-product synergy (example: infrastructure behind Gmail, Drive and Docs). (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]])
- API PMs work with developer communities as well as marketing and sales; an API platform is often the start of a marketplace. (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- Inbound (internal-facing) PM roles stress stakeholder work, consensus and saying no; internal customers can be harder than external ones because they have more access to the PM. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- The internal archetype is technically inclined, measured on cost savings, and good at build vs buy and internal tooling. (see [[2024-03-05-adam-fishman-pm-archetypes]])

## Contradictions

- The sources disagree on who a platform PM serves. Aha!, Agilemania and JustAnotherPM describe shared components for the company's own products (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]], [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]]). Lilia Gorbachik describes API PMs serving outside developer communities (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]]). Ally Mexicotte describes managing several products on one platform (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]]). Inference: these are three different jobs that share a title; ask which one a role means.
