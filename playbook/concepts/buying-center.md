# Buying center

## What it is

The people involved in a B2B purchase. PMA's roles: initiator (starts it), user, influencer (convinces others), decision maker (final approval), buyer (owns the budget), approver (often C-suite) and gatekeeper (can block it). PMA and Aventi cite Harvard Business Review: an average B2B purchase involves 6.8 people. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])

## How to use it

- Map each role's objectives and pain points, then write messaging per role: the economic buyer cares about cost and ROI, the end user about day-to-day usability (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- Build the GTM around whoever makes the buying decision, not just who uses the product (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- Map the buyer's journey: marketing owns awareness, sales takes over at decision (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])

## Watch out for

- Titles overlap in small organizations (PMA). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
- The 6.8 figure comes from 2017 CEB research reported in HBR; not verified in the batch. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]])
