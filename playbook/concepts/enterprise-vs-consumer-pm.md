# Enterprise (B2B) vs consumer (B2C) PM

## What it is

General knowledge: In B2B the buyer (who pays) and the user (who uses it) are usually different people, deals are large and slow, customers are few and loud, and sales, security, compliance and integrations shape the roadmap. In B2C the user is the buyer, there are many customers and little hand-holding, and the PM leans on usage data, experiments, design and brand. "Enterprise PM" is also used for a PM working inside a large company, which is a different thing.

## How to use it

- In B2B, map buyer, user and approver personas separately and talk to each.
- In B2B, keep a few reference customers close but don't let the biggest one set the roadmap.
- In B2C, invest in analytics and experiments; feedback comes from behavior more than conversations.

## Watch out for

- B2B roadmaps that become a list of promises made by sales.
- B2C teams that test small tweaks forever and avoid big bets.
- Reading "enterprise PM" in a job ad without checking which meaning it has.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Consumer PMs build for individuals with focus on UX, engagement and adoption at volume; enterprise PMs build for businesses with complex workflows, organizational buyers and contracts. (see [[2026-11-21-juan-types-of-pms-summary]])
- In B2C the user and customer are typically the same; in B2B the paying executive differs from the employee user, so the PM balances more stakeholders. Many employers prefer experience in the same model. (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- B2B PMs usually need domain knowledge, have fewer customers and higher prices, work closely with sales, and often make public roadmap commitments. (see [[online-school-types-of-product-manager]])
- Enterprise PMs focus on products for business customers, often B2B. (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]])
- Consumer PMs focus on consumer needs and UX; enterprise PMs handle complex features, long sales cycles and industry regulations. (see [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]])
- Consumer PMs refine customer-facing products such as e-commerce, social or mobile apps; enterprise PMs manage B2B products with long sales cycles such as ERP, security or HR software. (see [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]])
- Enterprise PMs build for business customers, move slowly because of legal, risk and procurement sign-off, and are unlikely at startups. Consumer PM is the most attractive and most competitive type. (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]])
- Enterprise PMs focus on scalability, security and integration with systems like CRM and ERP (example: Salesforce Marketing Cloud). (see [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]])
- An enterprise PM works inside a large organization: slower pace, red tape, many stakeholders, and integration with existing systems. (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]])
- An enterprise PM manages part of a big product with less independence and must coordinate with other PMs to avoid overlapping features. (see [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]])
- Enterprise PMs research and build features for enterprise clients with sales and customer support. (see [[2024-10-01-logrocket-types-of-pm-roles]])

## Contradictions

- The sources use "enterprise PM" for two different things. Aha!, Agilemania, Creately and JustAnotherPM mean a PM building for business customers (see [[aha-different-types-of-product-managers]], [[agilemania-types-of-product-managers]], [[2026-02-22-creately-15-types-of-pms]], [[2024-03-16-justanotherpm-5-types-of-pms]]). ProdPad and Lilia Gorbachik mean a PM inside a large organization (see [[2025-03-25-prodpad-12-types-of-pms]], [[2021-07-29-lilia-gorbachik-pm-types]]).
- Ally Mexicotte says you likely won't see enterprise PM roles at startups (see [[2023-01-23-ally-mexicotte-types-of-pms]]). Inference: that holds for the large-organization meaning, but many startups sell to businesses and hire B2B PMs; Recruiting from Scratch, for example, describes seed to Series C startups building engineering and AI products (see [[2026-06-25-recruiting-from-scratch-pm-job-titles]]).
