# PM archetypes

## What it is

General knowledge: An archetype describes the kind of PM a person is, not the job title: their background, instincts and the work they do best. Adam Fishman names seven: growth, UX-inclined, internal, general manager, optimizer, technician and white-spacer. Alex Mitchell names five: technical, analytic, marketing, get-stuff-done and visionary. Shreyas Doshi describes three leader archetypes: operator, craftsperson and visionary. These are practitioner models, not research.

## How to use it

- Score yourself and ask your manager to score you; compare the two.
- Match archetypes to the type of work (see [[types-of-product-work]]): white-spacers for zero-to-one, technicians for scaling, optimizers for core features.
- As a manager, balance archetypes on a team for the work ahead.

## Watch out for

- Using an archetype as a fixed label instead of a starting point for growth.
- Treating a blog model as a validated assessment.

## What the articles say

These are vendor, training-company, recruiter and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Seven archetypes, each with key skills, the work it is great at and the work it is less good at. Growth: experiments and speed. UX-inclined: user empathy and research. Internal: efficiency, build vs buy, cost savings. General manager: leadership, P&L, go-to-market. Optimizer: incremental quantitative improvement and messaging. Technician: scale and systems, often a former engineer or data scientist. White-spacer: vision, ambiguity, zero-to-one. (see [[2024-03-05-adam-fishman-pm-archetypes]])
- Maps the general manager to Doshi's operator and the white-spacer to Doshi's visionary; mentions Ravi Mehta's four PM shapes; says managers should load-balance archetypes across their team. (see [[2024-03-05-adam-fishman-pm-archetypes]])
- Mitchell's five archetypes: technical PM (former developer), analytic PM (former analyst), marketing PM, get-stuff-done PM (high-growth startups, delivery at all costs) and visionary PM (rare, thinks in years). (see [[stanford-online-types-of-pm-roles]])
- Lists the visionary PM as a rare job type focused on long-term strategy over day-to-day work. (see [[2025-03-05-specialist-pm-jobs-overview]])
