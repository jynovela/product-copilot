# Product strategy canvas (Perri)

## What it is

Melissa Perri's four-part structure for product strategy (2016), built on Toyota Kata. Vision: where the company or business line is going, qualitative. Challenge: the first business goal on the way to the vision. Target condition: smaller, measurable, achievable goals that break down the challenge; the team knows where to look but not exactly how to get there. Current state: today's measured reality against the target condition. Then the team runs the "Product Kata": find obstacles and experiment until it reaches the target condition. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])

## How to use it

- Split who sets what: vision by executives; challenge by the next level down; target conditions by managers with their teams, co-set as teams mature (Perri). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Measure the current state before work starts. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Perri's hypothetical Uber example: challenge, cut wait times in cities over 10 minutes to under 5; target condition, at least one driver per 50 people in each city. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- Shopify's eight-step strategy process borrows the current state and target condition terms. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])

## Watch out for

- Perri's example has inconsistent dates (challenge by 2018, target condition by 2017). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
- "Product Strategy is a system of achievable goals and visions"; Perri expects it to look like business strategy, because products exist to reach business objectives. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-strategy]])
