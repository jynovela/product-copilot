# Customer journey map

## What it is

General knowledge: A map of the stages a customer goes through, from first hearing of the product to using it and leaving or renewing. Each stage shows actions, touchpoints, thoughts, feelings and pain points. It makes the whole experience visible across teams.

## How to use it

- Build it from research, not from what the team believes.
- Mark the moments with the most pain or drop-off, and pick one to fix.

## Watch out for

- A pretty poster nobody uses.
- Mapping the ideal journey instead of the real one.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- A visual of all steps a customer takes with the product, "sometimes called the 5 Es"; helps PMs understand behavior and set strategy. (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]])
- Journey mapping is listed as a principle of Growth-Driven Design. (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]])

## Contradictions

- Neal Cabage calls journey maps "sometimes called the 5 Es" (see [[neal-cabage-21-pm-frameworks]]). Techreviewer uses "5 Es" for a marketing campaign method (see [[techreviewer-popular-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: the 5 Es (entice, enter, engage, exit, extend) is one specific journey model, not another name for all journey maps.
