# Stage-Gate

## What it is

General knowledge: Robert Cooper's model. Work moves through stages, and at each gate leaders decide go, kill, hold or recycle against set criteria. The classic version has discovery followed by five stages: scoping, building the business case, development, testing and validation, and launch. It suits costly, regulated or hardware products. Critics say it is slow and front-loads plans before learning, so many teams now run agile work inside the stages.

## How to use it

- Use gates to make real kill decisions, not as rubber stamps.
- Set gate criteria in advance.
- Run discovery and agile delivery inside stages where you can.

## Watch out for

- Gates that never kill anything.
- Heavy documents written to pass gates instead of to learn.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Five stages: discovery, scoping, development, testing and validation, launch and commercialization; gate outcomes are go, pause, revisit or kill; examples from Apple and Ford. (see [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]])
- Good for complex or high-cost products; structured phases with formal review checkpoints; slower than Agile. (see [[product-development-process-guide]])

## Contradictions

- Product School lists five stages without a business case stage (see [[2024-09-09-product-school-8-pm-frameworks]]). General knowledge: Cooper's classic model has discovery plus five stages, including building the business case. The six-stage process guide (see [[product-development-process-guide]]) is a different, generic linear model.
