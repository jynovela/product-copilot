# Good-better-best pricing

## What it is

Offer several packages at different price points and feature sets, such as free, pro and enterprise. Also called tiered pricing or price lining. Named by Nationwide (citing Rafi Mohammed, HBR 2018), Product School, Shopify, Gupta, Qualtrics and LogRocket. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Use it when customers range widely in needs (LogRocket). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- The lowest and highest tiers make the middle look like the best value (Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Three options make people choose between your plans instead of whether to buy. Peep Laja's beer test (no study cited): adding a cheaper or dearer third option moved most buyers to a different middle choice. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Choice paralysis and harder forecasting (Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Paddle says good-better-best is "dying out" in software; Gupta, Shopify, Product School and Qualtrics treat it as a staple. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Which tier buyers pick: ProductPlan says most pick the cheapest, Product School says the middle, Qualtrics says tiers anchor buyers to the middle. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
