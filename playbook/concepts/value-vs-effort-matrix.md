# Value vs effort matrix

## What it is

General knowledge: Place each idea on two axes: value to customers and the business, and effort to build. High value and low effort are quick wins; high value and high effort are big bets to plan; low value and low effort are fill-ins; low value and high effort are to avoid. Also called value vs complexity.

## How to use it

- Use it for a first pass over a long list, then score the survivors more carefully.
- Agree on what value means for this quarter before scoring.

## Watch out for

- Estimates are guesses and depend on who's in the room.
- Consistency breaks down across many teams.

## What the framework articles say

These are vendor and practitioner articles, not neutral sources.

- Calls it "value vs. effect" and labels four quadrants: do first, do second, do last, avoid. (see [[atlassian-6-prioritization-frameworks]])
- Fast initial triage; scoring debates expose strategy gaps; estimates are subjective and inconsistent at scale; pair with RICE afterwards. (see [[tempo-9-prioritization-frameworks]])
- Lists it as value vs complexity on a prioritization matrix. (see [[productplan-37-pm-frameworks]])
- A fast visual way to spot quick wins and deprioritize low-value, high-effort work. (see [[2026-11-20-juan-pm-frameworks-list]])

## Contradictions

- Atlassian says value runs up the vertical axis and effort runs left to right, then places high value/high effort in the upper left and low value/high effort in the lower left (see [[atlassian-6-prioritization-frameworks]]). With those axes, high effort is on the right. Its quadrant positions contradict its own axes; the labels (do first, do second, do last, avoid) are still usable.
