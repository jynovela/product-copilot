# Concierge and Wizard of Oz tests

## What it is

Two minimal products that deliver real value without building the automation. Concierge: a manual, high-touch service and the customer knows it's manual. Wizard of Oz: it looks automated but a person does the work, and the user doesn't know (Huryn). Related: piecemeal or mash-up products stitched from existing tools and no-code. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])

## How to use it

- Use when you need to learn whether the outcome is valuable before investing in how it is produced. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- Run with a handful of real customers and record what they do, not what they say. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])

## Watch out for

- Costlier than pretotypes because you deliver real value (Huryn). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
- Inference: a Wizard of Oz test raises honesty questions if users would object to a hidden human; decide disclosure upfront.

## Contradictions

- Product School treats them as one thing ("Wizard of Oz… (concierge MVPs)"); Huryn separates them by whether the user knows a human is doing the work. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-experiments]])
