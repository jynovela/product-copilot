# Value metric

## What it is

The unit you charge by, such as seats, contacts, data or usage. Paddle (a vendor, the most detailed source): "get the value metric right and you'll do ok". A good value metric grows with the value the customer gets. Examples given: HubSpot and MailChimp charge on contacts, AWS on data, Rolls-Royce on engine use. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- List 5 to 10 candidate metrics and test them with customers (Paddle). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Pick it first: Paddle's order of work is segments and value metric, then rough price level, positioning and packaging, then add-ons, exact price, localization and discounts, then freemium and new markets. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Start with the order of magnitude ($10 vs $100), not $500 vs $505 (Paddle). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- Gupta lists a misaligned value metric among the top three pricing mistakes. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Paddle's claim that value-metric companies grow about 2x with half the churn comes from its own data and isn't checked. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Paddle calls seats a "relic"; ProdPad and Slack's model treat per-seat as standard, with AI as an add-on tier. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
