# Freemium and free trials

## What it is

Freemium gives a free basic tier with paid upgrades (Spotify, Canva, Dropbox, Slack are the examples given). A free trial gives time-limited access; a reverse trial starts the user on the paid tier and drops them to free (ProdPad). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## How to use it

- Make sure there is a must-have paid feature, or conversion stays low (Qualtrics). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Plan onboarding, in-product education and upgrade prompts as part of go-to-market when the model depends on conversion (Product School; see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-go-to-market]]).
- Rough conversion ranges, from vendor figures compiled 2006 to 2016 by Ada Chen Rekhi: freemium 1 to 10%, trial without a card up to about 25%, trial with a card 30 to 50%. Old; treat as rough. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Watch out for

- The cost of serving free users, low conversion and churn (Qualtrics and others). (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
- Paddle (a vendor) says the best companies wait 2 to 3 years before adding freemium. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])

## Contradictions

- Paddle says freemium is "an acquisition model, not a part of pricing"; most other sources treat it as a pricing model. (see [[2026-11-24-research-digest-pricing]])
