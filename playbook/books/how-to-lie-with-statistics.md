# How to Lie with Statistics

- **Author:** Darrell Huff
- **Topics:** [[decisions-and-data]]
- **On the list:** [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]]

## Why it's on the list

- Being metrics-driven needs a base in statistics. This lively classic leaves you smarter and more skeptical. (see [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]])

## Key ideas

General knowledge: the points below come from model memory, not from an ingested copy of the book. Check details before quoting or relying on them.

- Biased samples produce confident, wrong conclusions. See [[statistical-traps]].
- "Average" can mean mean, median or mode; the choice can change the story. See [[statistical-traps]].
- Truncated axes and picture charts exaggerate small differences. See [[statistical-traps]].
- Correlation is not causation; watch for the semi-attached figure that proves something different from what's claimed. See [[statistical-traps]].

## Use it when

- Reading an experiment result or dashboard
- Presenting metrics honestly
