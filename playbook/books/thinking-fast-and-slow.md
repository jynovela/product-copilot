# Thinking, Fast and Slow

- **Author:** Daniel Kahneman
- **Topics:** [[decisions-and-data]]
- **On the list:** [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]]

## Why it's on the list

When does the fast, instinctive brain decide before the analytical one weighs in? One of the most important psychology books ever written. (see [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]])

## Key ideas

General knowledge: the points below come from model memory, not from an ingested copy of the book. Check details before quoting or relying on them.

- System 1 is fast, automatic and intuitive; System 2 is slow, effortful and deliberate. See [[system-1-and-system-2]].
- Heuristics like anchoring, availability and representativeness bias judgment. See [[system-1-and-system-2]].
- Losses loom larger than gains (prospect theory), and framing changes choices. See [[system-1-and-system-2]].
- The planning fallacy: we underestimate time and cost; use the outside view (reference classes) and premortems. See [[premortem]].

## Use it when

- Estimating timelines
- Designing choices and pricing
- Checking your own judgment
