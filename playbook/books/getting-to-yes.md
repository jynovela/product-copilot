# Getting to Yes

- **Author:** Roger Fisher and William Ury
- **Topics:** [[negotiation-feedback-and-relationships]]
- **On the list:** [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]]

## Why it's on the list

- PMs must be strong negotiators, and this classic is the best guide to negotiation. (see [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]])

## Key ideas

General knowledge: the points below come from model memory, not from an ingested copy of the book. Check details before quoting or relying on them.

- Separate the people from the problem. See [[principled-negotiation]].
- Focus on interests, not positions: ask why someone wants what they ask for. See [[principled-negotiation]].
- Invent options for mutual gain before deciding. See [[principled-negotiation]].
- Insist on objective criteria, and know your BATNA (best alternative to a negotiated agreement). See [[principled-negotiation]].

- Roadmap negotiations with sales or executives
- Scoping with engineering
- Partner deals
