## Core argument

Product-market fit means meeting real customer needs better than the
alternatives, and it breaks into five layers: target customer, underserved
needs, value proposition, feature set and UX. Make an explicit hypothesis
for each layer, bottom up, then test the cheapest artifact that checks them
with customers, in waves, fixing the lowest broken layer first. Qualitative
work defines the product; after launch, retention curves show fit, and
analytics and A/B tests optimize one metric at a time. (see
[[2015-06-01-dan-olsen-the-lean-product-playbook]])

## What it adds

- One end-to-end sequence from customer to launch and optimization, with a
  worksheet or grid at each step.
- Formulas for customer value and opportunity from importance and
  satisfaction ratings.
- A value proposition grid built on Kano categories and competitors.
- The clearest split between MVPs and MVP tests, with a 2×2 of test types.
- Practical moderated user-testing mechanics: wave size, script, timing,
  questions, and a table for tracking issues across waves.
- Retention curve parameters and a default order for which metric to fix.

Weaker on: business models and pricing (touched only through metrics),
B2B buying and sales, and evidence. Most support is the author's own
consulting and company stories. Its discovery interviews ask people to
rate the value of hypothetical benefits, which [[mom-test-interviews]]
warns against. The agile and QA chapter is a general overview.
