## Core argument

Companies fall into the build trap when they measure success by output
instead of the value exchanged with customers. Escaping it takes a
product-led organization: PMs who connect business goals to customer
problems, a strategy deployed as outcome goals at each level so teams can
decide the how, a repeatable process (the Product Kata) for reaching each
goal, and rewards, budgets and reviews that run on outcomes. (see
[[2020-11-17-swamy-seetharaman-escaping-the-build-trap-summary]])

## What it adds

- The clearest account on the list of how strategy cascades from vision to
  team experiments, with the three strategic gaps and their wrong fixes.
- A named set of PM failure modes (mini-CEO, waiter, former project
  manager) and a level-by-level PM career path.
- A concrete review cadence and roadmap format built around outcomes.
- The first detailed job description for product operations.
- A six-question test for whether a company is product-led.

Weaker on: discovery and experiment technique, which other books cover in
more depth ([[continuous-discovery-habits]], [[the-lean-product-playbook]]).
Most evidence is consulting stories and a fictional company. Through the
summaries we can't see the book's caveats or examples, and some wording
may be the summarizers'.
