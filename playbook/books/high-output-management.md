# High Output Management

- **Author:** Andrew S. Grove
- **Topics:** [[leadership-and-management]], [[decisions-and-data]]
- **On the list:** [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]] (Ken Norton top pick)

## Why it's on the list

- It only gets better with age. Grove first introduced OKRs here, and his advice on meetings and 1-on-1s shaped Ken's own writing. (see [[2026-01-07-ken-norton-best-pm-books]])

## Key ideas

General knowledge: the points below come from model memory, not from an ingested copy of the book. Check details before quoting or relying on them.

- A manager's output is the output of their team plus the teams they influence. See [[managerial-leverage]].
- Spend time on high-leverage activities: ones that affect many people or a long period. See [[managerial-leverage]].
- Objectives and key results: what we want, and measurable signs we're getting there. See [[okrs]].
- Regular 1-on-1s, driven by the report's agenda, are among the highest-leverage things a manager does. See [[one-on-ones]].
- Pair indicators so that optimizing one (e.g. speed) doesn't silently hurt another (e.g. quality). See [[statistical-traps]].
- Adapt management style to task-relevant maturity: more structure for new tasks, more freedom as experience grows.
