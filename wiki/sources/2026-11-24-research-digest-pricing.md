- **Kind:** other (research digest)

## Summary

About 40 articles on pricing (plus three found the same day), condensed into one file: the cost, competition and value foundations, about 15 named pricing strategies, pricing models and the value metric, willingness-to-pay research methods, process frameworks, how to change prices, who owns pricing, 19 conflicts between sources, and a flagged table of numbers. Most sources are vendors or course sellers.

Every claim in the raw file names the article it came from, and vendor claims, unsourced numbers and errors are flagged. Treat it as a map of what public sources say, not as fact, and never as evidence about this product's customers.

## Key points

- Cost sets the floor, customer value the ceiling, competitors sit in between (section 1.1).
- Value-based pricing is called the default for B2B SaaS by most software sources; cost-plus is called the most common overall (sections 1.2 to 1.4, conflict 9).
- The value metric (the unit you charge by) is the first pricing choice in Paddle's order of work (sections 1.16, 1.17).
- Conjoint, Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger and A/B tests are the willingness-to-pay methods; sources disagree on which is best (section 1.18, conflict 1).
- Two sources confuse markup with margin, and one swaps the Economist decoy prices; the digest records the correct versions (conflicts 13, 14).
