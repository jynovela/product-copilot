- **Kind:** other (research digest)

## Summary

About 75 articles on general goal setting, product goals and OKRs condensed into one file: goal-quality acronyms, goal-setting theory, types of goals, goal hierarchies (Pichler, EOS, 4DX, MBO, Balanced Scorecard), step lists, OKR structure, committed vs aspirational OKRs, scoring, traps and tests, OKRs in product work, process and cadence, 15 agreed practices, conflicts, worked examples and flagged statistics. It is strong on OKR mechanics and weak on evidence: most statistics are vendor-cited.

Every claim in the raw file names the article it came from, and vendor claims, unsourced numbers and errors are flagged. Treat it as a map of what public sources say, not as fact, and never as evidence about this product's customers.

## Key points

- Goals describe outcomes, not outputs; keep the work (initiatives) apart from the measure (practice 1).
- Committed goals should be hit; aspirational goals are expected to land near 70% (sections 1.7, 1.8).
- Google's six OKR traps and litmus tests are the most reusable checks (section 1.9).
- Pair growth goals with guardrails; the LedgerPulse example shows how a shortcut games activation (section 5.1).
- Many vendor example OKRs break the vendor's own outcome rule (section 5.3).
