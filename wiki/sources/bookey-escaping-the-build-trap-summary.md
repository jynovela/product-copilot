## Summary

Short section summaries for each chapter, followed by quiz-style questions
and answers. Thinner than the Medium summary and written in generic
summary language, so pages use it for three things only: the book's
chapter numbers, points the Medium summary skips (the constraints on the
value exchange in ch2, team organization by value stream, feature or
component in ch9), and checking attributions such as Stephen Bungay's
definition of strategy (ch10, ch11). The raw copy is kept local.

Chapters of the book (numbering from its table of contents, as listed in
the Bookey summary): 1 The value exchange system, 2 Constraints on the
value exchange system, 3 Projects versus products versus services, 4 The
product-led organization, 5 What we know and what we don't, 6 Bad product
manager archetypes, 7 A great product manager, 8 The product manager career
path, 9 Organizing your teams, 10 What is strategy?, 11 Strategic gaps, 12
Creating a good strategic framework, 13 Company-level vision and strategic
intents, 14 Product vision and portfolio, 15 The Product Kata, 16
Understanding the direction and setting success metrics, 17 Problem
exploration, 18 Solution exploration, 19 Building and optimizing your
solution, 20 Outcome-focused communication, 21 Rewards and incentives, 22
Safety and learning, 23 Budgeting, 24 Customer centricity, 25 Marquetly:
the product-led company, then an afterword and an appendix (six questions
to tell whether a company is product-led). Method pages cite
`Build Trap chN` with the book's chapter numbers.

## Key points

- Customer-side constraints are mostly outside the company's control; business-side constraints (people, process, policy, strategy, culture) are not. See [[build-trap]]
- Teams organized by feature drift to output, and teams organized by technical component optimize the component; organize by value stream. See [[strategy-deployment]]
- Bias: commercial summary; generic and sometimes vague. No quotes taken from it.
