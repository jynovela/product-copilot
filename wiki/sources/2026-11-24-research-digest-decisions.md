## Summary

About 37 pieces on decision making condensed into one file: decision processes, who decides (RAPID, DACI, RACI, SPADE, Pichler's decision rules, MIT models, Vroom-Yetton-Jago), classifying decisions, tools for evaluating options, risk and revisiting (premortem, kill criteria, reopening tests), AI as a co-thinker, definitions and biases, 9 agreed practices and 9 conflicts.

Every claim in the raw file names the article it came from, and vendor claims, unsourced numbers and errors are flagged. Treat it as a map of what public sources say, not as fact, and never as evidence about this product's customers.

## Key points

- Define the decision and who decides before debating options (practices 1, 3).
- Generate more than two real options, including doing nothing (practice 2).
- Match the process to the decision: heavy process only for irreversible, consequential calls (practice 4).
- Approver, Responsible and Agree mean different things in DACI, SPADE, RACI and RAPID (section 2).
- Sources disagree on one decider vs group rules, and on how hard it should be to reopen a decision (conflicts 1, 4).
