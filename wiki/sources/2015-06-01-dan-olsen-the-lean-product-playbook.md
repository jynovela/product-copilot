## Summary

Olsen's six-step Lean Product Process for reaching product-market fit, and
how to optimize a product with analytics after launch. Chapters: 1
Achieving product-market fit, 2 Problem space versus solution space, 3
Determine your target customer, 4 Identify underserved needs, 5 Define your
value proposition, 6 Specify your MVP feature set, 7 Create your MVP
prototype, 8 Apply the principles of great UX design, 9 Test your MVP with
customers, 10 Iterate and pivot, 11 A worked case study, 12 Build your
product using Agile development, 13 Measure your key metrics, 14 Use
analytics to optimize, 15 Conclusion. Method pages cite it as
`Lean Product Playbook chN`. The raw file is copyrighted and stays local;
pages paraphrase it.

## Key points

- Fit has five layers, climbed bottom up; fix the lowest broken layer first; pivot when target customers stay lukewarm. See [[product-market-fit-pyramid]]
- Keep needs (the what) apart from designs (the how); customers react better to solutions than to abstract needs. See [[problem-vs-solution-space]]
- Segment by needs and behavior, separate users from buyers, write one-page personas. See [[target-customer-and-personas]]
- Write needs as precise benefits and ladder them. See [[customer-benefits]]
- Score needs by importance and satisfaction; value = I × S, opportunity = I × (1 − S). See [[opportunity-scoring]]
- State where you'll win with a must-have, performance and delighter grid against competitors. See [[value-proposition-grid]]
- Chunk features and rank by value over effort; v1 takes all must-haves plus the chosen differentiators. See [[mvp-feature-set]]
- Product or marketing, qualitative or quantitative: a 2×2 of MVP tests. See [[mvp-test-matrix]]
- Build UX through four layers; more effort means fewer users act. See [[ux-design-iceberg]]
- Test in waves of five to eight, one user at a time; track issues and ratings across waves. See [[moderated-user-testing]]
- Retention curves are the best measure of fit. See [[product-market-fit-measures]]
- Improve one metric at a time, usually retention, then conversion, then acquisition. See [[metric-that-matters-most]]

## Quotes

- "The more user effort required to take an action, the lower the percentage of users who will take that action." (ch8)
- "Retention rate is the single best metric to measure your product-market fit." (ch13)
- "qualitative helps you define your product and quantitative helps you optimize your product." (ch14)
