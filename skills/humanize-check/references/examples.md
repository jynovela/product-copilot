# Before and after

One pair per tell. The first ten are the tics Juan named. Each "After"
keeps the facts of the "Before" and says the point directly. The check
script's tests read this file: every "Before" must trigger its heading's
check, and every "After" must come back clean.

## Juan's tics

### negation-first-reveal

- Before: It is not a wrong answer. It is a non-answer.
- After: The answer names no number and no owner, so nobody can act on it.

### significance-designation

- Before: Churn by plan is the number that actually answers the question.
- After: Churn by plan answers the question: teams on the Starter plan leave twice as often.

### abstraction-agency

- Before: The table shows that onboarding drops off at step three.
- After: In the table, 40% of new teams stop at step three of onboarding.

### deferred-noun

- Before: Five of the six requests came from agencies. The sixth is not.
- After: Five of the six requests came from agencies and one came from an in-house team.

### coy-header

- Before: ## What "activated" means
- After: ## Definition of an activated team

### aphoristic-line

- Before: It is the kind of number that looks like evidence and is not.
- After: Three interviews can't show how common the problem is.

### straw-man

- Before: "Users want more features" is the obvious reading, and it is wrong.
- After: Users asked for faster exports in 9 of 12 calls, and for new features in 2.

### fragment-cadence

- Before: Six interviews. One segment. One week. One question.
- After: We ran six interviews in one week, all with small agencies, all on the same question about approvals.

### dressed-metaphor

- Before: That is information loss wearing the costume of a style fix.
- After: The rewrite dropped two numbers while it fixed the style.

### label-first

- Before: The left column of this table.
- After: Look at the left column of the table, which lists each plan's price.

## Other tells

### dash

- Before: We shipped the export — and nobody used it.
- After: We shipped the export, and nobody used it in the first month.

### contrast-formula

- Before: This is not just a pricing change, but a new strategy.
- After: The price change moves us toward larger teams, which is a strategy change.

### setup-colon

- Before: The result: approvals took two days longer.
- After: Approvals took two days longer.

### ai-word

- Before: We will leverage the new API to deliver a seamless experience.
- After: We will use the new API so imports finish without a manual step.

### puffery

- Before: The dashboard plays a vital role in how teams plan.
- After: Teams open the dashboard in 80% of planning meetings.

### empty-ing-tail

- Before: Usage grew 20% last month, highlighting the value of the feature.
- After: Usage grew 20% last month after the feature launched.

### vague-authority

- Before: Studies show that onboarding emails raise activation.
- After: Our March test raised activation from 30% to 34% with two onboarding emails.

### glue-transition

- Before: Moreover, the feature reduced support tickets.
- After: The feature also cut support tickets from 50 to 35 a week.

### filler

- Before: It's important to note that the beta ends Friday.
- After: The beta ends Friday.

### stacked-hedge

- Before: This could potentially slow down approvals.
- After: This may slow down approvals by a day.

### false-balance

- Before: Both sides have merit, and the team should discuss further.
- After: I'd keep the free tier, because 30% of paid teams started on it.

### chatbot-residue

- Before: I hope this helps! Let me know if you have questions.
- After: Send me the March numbers and I'll update the report.

### semicolon

- Before: The beta went well; we plan to launch in May.
- After: The beta went well. We plan to launch in May.

### rule-of-three

- Before: The plan needs clarity, consistency, and accountability.
- After: The plan needs one owner per goal.

### title-case-heading

- Before: ## Next Steps For The Launch Team
- After: ## Next steps for the launch team

## Structure

### bold-lead-list

- Before: - **A morning brief.** It lists what's due today.\n- **A memory.** Every claim links to its source.
- After: Each morning it lists what's due today. Every claim it makes links to its source.

### colon-lead-in

- Before: Three habits keep the picture trustworthy:\n\n- It never guesses a number.
- After: It never guesses a number, so every figure on a page comes from a source.

### x-not-y

- Before: Both files hold the same facts, so it's an annoyance, not an error.
- After: Both files hold the same facts, so you lose nothing if the link opens the wrong one.
