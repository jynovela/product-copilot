# Type: interview guide

`doc_type: interview-guide`. Humanize mode: light.

**Reader:** the person running the customer calls. **They decide:** what
to ask, and whether a call taught them something.

## Read first

- The problem page or pages the calls are about: what Evidence and Size
  already show, and what they leave unknown.
- The customer and account pages, for who to talk to.
- The experiment page, when the interviews are a test with a pass and fail
  rule set in advance.
- The playbook's interview method pages that exist, found by name:
  `mom-test-interviews`, `interview-bad-data`, `commitment-and-advancement`,
  `customer-slicing`, `finding-customer-conversations` and
  `customer-learning-process`. They are advice, not evidence.

## Sections

1. **Learning goals.** One to three questions, each tied to a gap on
   a problem page or an experiment's assumption.
2. **Who to talk to.** The segment slice, specific enough to find people,
   and two or three screening questions.
3. **Opening.** Thirty seconds on why you're calling, without describing
   the idea.
4. **Questions.** Eight to twelve, grouped: their context, the last time
   the problem happened, what they did about it, and what it cost them.
   Every question is about their past behavior.
5. **Don't ask.** Questions to avoid for this topic, each with the better
   version: "Would you use a tool that...?" becomes "When did you last
   ...? What did you do?"
6. **Listen for.** Commitment and real cost (time, money, workarounds
   built), and the bad data to discount: compliments, generic claims
   ("I always..."), and promises about the future.
7. **Close.** Ask for a concrete next step: an introduction, a follow-up,
   a trial, a payment.
8. **After the call.** Save the notes or transcript in `raw/` the same day,
   ingest them, then ask for an interview summary.

## Type rules

- Every question must pass three tests. It's about their life, not our
  idea. It's about a specific past event. And "yes, I'd use that" can't
  answer it.
- No leading questions and no pitching, including in the opening.
